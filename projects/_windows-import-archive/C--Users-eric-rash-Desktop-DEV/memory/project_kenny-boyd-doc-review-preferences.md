Kenny Boyd (reviewer on the Baylor Athletics Software and Technology Management
Framework) consistently pushes back on three things in draft policy language:

1. **No language that implies the department was previously incompetent.** He cut
   "so the department stops discovering it is in the final year of an agreement
   with no time to act" for exactly this reason. Rewrite as forward-looking
   capability ("advance visibility of contract end dates and the lead time to
   evaluate options"), not as a problem being fixed.
2. **No judgmental or ranking-flavored labels.** He rejected the Light / Standard /
   Elevated review tiers. Neutral, descriptive, escalating names work better —
   Level 1 Standard / Level 2 Enhanced / Level 3 Comprehensive Review. He floated
   Basic / Intermediate / Advanced, which reads as sophistication of the
   *technology* rather than depth of *review*.
3. **Name the seniority of the reviewer, not just the office.** For ITS he wanted
   it clear that a supervisory-level person with authority to commit resources is
   involved, not a line technician. Frame this as decision authority the request
   requires, never as competence of the staff.

He also wants Baylor branding carried through headers/footers and draft status
marked on every page.

**Why:** These are tone constraints, not content ones — the same facts land or
fail on phrasing. Getting them wrong costs a full review cycle.

**How to apply:** Before sending any Athletics policy or framework draft for his
review, scan for blame-implying rationale, ranking adjectives on process tiers,
and unqualified office names where seniority matters.
