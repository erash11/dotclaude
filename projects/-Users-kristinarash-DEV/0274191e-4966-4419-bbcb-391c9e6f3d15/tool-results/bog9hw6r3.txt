    14	# Nothing Works Forever: Programs Have a Shelf Life
    15	
    16	**What it is** — Slide 3 (headed "Horse/Carrot Theory"): "ALL programs work-they only work for so long-NOTHING works forever!" and "To adapt is not to adapt (Louie Simmons)." A third bullet reads only "Definition of adaptation" and the slide gives no definition (slides 4-5 supply one, see [[claim--adaptation-as-active-disequilibrium-and-the-decks-supercompensation-wording]]). The deck never explains the "Horse/Carrot" title. Slide 16 (Conclusion): "Once the body stops learning the body stops growing," "How you perform or present an exercise to the body is oft times more important than the actual exercise," and "there are many means and methods. Use them all!"
    17	
    18	**Why it works** — The deck gives no mechanism and no data for the shelf-life claim. The nearest stated reason is slide 4's bullet that adaptation "is also NOT an equilibrium process (Verkoshansky)." Inference: if the body keeps adapting to a stimulus, the same stimulus eventually stops being a demand, which is the reading the slide invites but does not state.
    19	
    20	**When to apply** — When planning how often to change exercises, methods or loading patterns. Eric's baseline: undulating or non-linear periodization, waving volume week to week, is his stated preference "to avoid staleness while still building tolerance for higher loads" (`docs/method-baseline.md`, line 106, Explicit, interview 2026-09-28, Q1). Inference: that preference fits this slide's direction, but the deck does not describe a waving model itself, only 4-week deload examples (slide 11).
    21	
    22	**When NOT to apply** — The deck gives no period for "so long" and no sign for when a program has stopped working. Slide 3 ("ALL programs work") sits against slide 6 ("ALL programs are flawed"), see [[claim--nobody-has-it-figured-out-versus-the-decks-own-prescriptive-weekly-template]]. Slide 16's "once the body stops learning" is not defined. Confidence is untested: a practitioner's statement with a quoted attribution to Louie Simmons and no evidence.
    23	
    24	**Example** — None given. The deck offers no case of a program that stopped working.

    14	# Adaptation as Active Disequilibrium, Beside the Deck's Supercompensation Wording
    15	
    16	**What it is** — Two frames appear in the deck.
    17	- Slide 4: "Adaptation is also NOT an equilibrium process (Verkoshansky)." Slide 5: adaptation "Is a phenomenon of accommodation," "characterizes the organism's entire reaction," "reflects the specific features of the external influences," and "is the active maintenance of a definite level of disequilibrium between the organism and the environment." Slide 5 credits the last bullet, "The fundamental reason for the origin and development of accommodative reconstruction within the organism," to Verkhoshansky (spelling as printed).
    18	- Supercompensation wording: slide 2 calls the body "self-regulating and supercompensating." Slide 7: "The optimal load applied during a period of supercompensation (deload week) creates a summation/ additive affect that aids in stabilizing the increase in performance." Slide 9: "take advantage of supercompensation."
    19	
    20	**Why it works** — The deck gives no mechanism for either frame and never says the two differ. Inference: slide 7 uses the classic recover-then-rise picture, while slides 4-5 name a source (Verkhoshansky) whose co-authored book in this library argues "adaptive reconstruction" is a better term than supercompensation.
    21	
    22	**When to apply** — Treat the deck's deload advice as practice, not as support for any one adaptation theory. Eric's baseline: no stated position on supercompensation versus adaptive reconstruction in `docs/method-baseline.md`.
    23	
    24	**When NOT to apply** — Marked contested: the deck uses the supercompensation word (slides 2, 7, 9) while citing a source (slides 4-5) that this library records as critiquing it (see the Related entries); the deck does not acknowledge the tension. Do not cite slide 7's "period of supercompensation" as evidence that supercompensation occurs.
