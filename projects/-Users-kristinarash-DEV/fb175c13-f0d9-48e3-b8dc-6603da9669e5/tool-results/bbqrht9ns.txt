-**Also in 9.1** — Verkhoshansky's own paper restates the two layers in 9.1 §1.1, "Understanding compensatory adaptation" (PDF 521 / p.510, checked against the page image). The compensatory mechanisms are "dynamic and quickly rising physiological means of breakdown in order to provide for the body in extreme conditions and to gradually extinguish these means according to the amount of development of stable long term adaptation," so "they can be looked at as pre-adaptation appearances (Kusnetzov, 68)." Long-term adaptation "can have a progressive character if the training influence is stressful and brings about systematic disturbance of body homeostasis," and if the restructuring is "sufficiently substantial." The mechanism of the transition is in [[claim--acute-adaptation-runs-at-the-limit-and-long-term-adaptation-needs-protein-synthesis]].
