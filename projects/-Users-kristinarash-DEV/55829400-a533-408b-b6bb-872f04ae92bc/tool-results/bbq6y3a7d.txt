    27	## 1. The central requirement: zero weekly hand-edits
    28	
    29	PRODUCT.md makes this a hard requirement, and it is the requirement the prototype
    30	does **not** meet. The prototype hardcodes every weekly variable. Production must
    31	derive all of them. This is the single most important section of this spec.
    32	
    33	### 1.1 What is hardcoded today, and what replaces it
    34	
    35	| Prototype constant | Where | Production derivation |
    36	|---|---|---|
    37	| `YEAR = 2025` | `pull_snapshot.py` | `season = now.year if now.month >= 8 else now.year - 1` |
    38	| `WEEK = 6` | `pull_snapshot.py`, `build_payloads.py` | The most recent **completed** regular-season game: from `/games?year&team`, keep `seasonType == "regular"` and `homePoints is not None`, take `max(week)` |
    39	| `TEAM = "Baylor"` | both | The one legitimate constant. Lives in `config.py`. |
    40	| `OPP = "Kansas State"` | both | Read off the resolved game row: whichever of `homeTeam`/`awayTeam` is not `TEAM` |
    41	| `NEXT_OPP = "TCU"` | `pull_snapshot.py` | First scheduled game with `week > resolved_week`, `seasonType == "regular"` |
    42	| `GAME_ID = 401756911` | `pull_snapshot.py` | `id` of the resolved game row |
    43	| `BAY_TID = "118"` | `build_payloads.py` | `select franchise_id from teams where is_baylor limit 1` — `pull_snapshot.py` already runs this query and then ignores the answer |
    44	| `gr.season = 2025` | SQL in `build_payloads.py` | Bound parameter from the resolved season |
    45	| `WEEK` in SQL | SQL in both | Bound parameter from the resolved week |
    46	
    47	**Gate:** a `grep` test in CI/pre-flight fails the build if a four-digit year, a
    48	week number, or any Big-12 opponent name appears as a literal in `templates/` or in
    49	any module other than `config.py`. See §6.
    50	
    51	### 1.2 Week resolution edge cases (must be handled, not assumed)
    52	
    53	| Case | Behaviour |
    54	|---|---|
    55	| Bye week (no completed game since the last report) | Exit with a clear message, produce no PDF. A bye week report is not a product. |
    56	| Postseason row mixed into a season query | Filtered out. See §1.3. |
    57	| Fewer than 3 prior weeks of player snaps | The flag rule needs 3 points. Players with fewer get `band: null` and `flag: null`, and the sheet says how many players were excluded and why. Never silently dropped. |
    58	| No next opponent (last game of the season) | `meta.next_game = "No further games"` — already handled in the prototype, keep it. |
    59	| CFBD API down or returning partial data | Fail loudly before rendering. Never publish a sheet with a missing panel. |
    60	
    61	### 1.3 The `seasonType` filter — mandate it once
    62	
    63	CFBD mixes a postseason row into a season query and relabels it with a
    64	regular-season week number, producing a phantom duplicate week. The prototype filters
    65	this in only two places (`tempo_rows`, `box_snaps`); `schedule`, `epa_by_week` and
    66	`season_tempo` are unfiltered and carry the bug.
    67	
    68	**Production rule: the filter is applied once, in `sources/cfbd.py`, at the moment
    69	each list is fetched.** Nothing downstream re-filters, and nothing downstream is
    70	allowed to see a postseason row. This is a data-layer responsibility, not a
    71	presentation one.

   114	## 3. The data contract
   115	
   116	The payload is the frozen interface between Python and the templates. A template may
   117	only print what the payload contains. This is what makes "zero hand-edits" checkable.
   118	
   119	### 3.1 `meta` — every weekly variable, derived once
   120	
   121	Shared identically by both editions. Keys, all required:
   122	
   123	```
   124	team, opponent, next_opponent, season, week,
   125	date            "October 4, 2025"        date_short   "04 Oct 2025"
   126	venue, team_score, opp_score, result ("W"/"L"), home (bool), excitement,
   127	next_week, next_home,
   128	next_game       "Week 8 · at TCU"        next_short   "Wk 8 at TCU"
   129	week_label      "week 6"
   130	season_range    "weeks 1–6"
   131	baseline_range  "weeks 1–5"              baseline_range_prose  "weeks 1 to 5"
   132	```
   133	
   134	No opponent name, date, week number or season label may be typed into markup. The
   135	same rule covers computed prose: a claim like "the widest gap of the season" is
   136	generated from the rows rendered beside it.
   137	
   138	### 3.2 Coaches edition (`payload_a.json`)
   139	
   140	| Key | Shape | Source |
   141	|---|---|---|
   142	| `bands` | one row per metric, 14 metrics | `metrics.band_rows(baylor_season_adv)` |
   143	| `next_bands` | same shape, next opponent | `metrics.band_rows(next_opp_season_adv)` |
   144	| `winprob` | `{p, wp, hs, as}` per play | CFBD `/metrics/wp` |
   145	| `score_events` | plays where the score changed, with WP swing | derived from `winprob` |
   146	| `box`, `ppa_game`, `ppa_season`, `epa_by_week` | as prototype | CFBD |
   147	| `drive_log`, `tempo` | as prototype | CFBD `/drives` |
   148	| `box_snaps` | `{off, def, off_drives, def_drives}` | advanced box, regular season only |
   149	| `pff_offense`, `pff_defense` | top 11 by grade, `snaps >= 15` | PFF |
   150	| `p4_n` | Power-Four team count behind the percentiles | CFBD |
   151	
   152	A `bands` row: `key, label, side, unit, direction, value, p10, p25, p50, p75, p90,
   153	lo, hi, pctl, flag`. `direction` is `+1` higher-is-better, `-1` lower-is-better;
   154	`pctl` is already polarity-corrected so the template never re-orients an axis.
   155	
   156	### 3.3 Performance edition (`payload_b.json`)
   157	
   158	Everything in §3.1, plus `tempo`, `snaps_by_quarter`, `drive_log`, `season_tempo`,
   159	`next_opp_tempo`, `group_totals`, `weeks`, `flag_rule`, `season_players`.
   160	
   161	**`box_snaps` is copied from payload A, never recomputed.** One spine per quantity —
   162	two editions computing the same number separately is how they drifted apart the first
   163	time.
   164	
   165	A `season_players` row: `name, position, group, side, by_week, total, games,
   166	this_week, baseline, band, delta_pct, dev, flag`.
   167	
   168	**The flag rule** (moves to `flags.py` unchanged — DESIGN.md documents the reasoning,
   169	and it must stay identical or the design doc becomes a lie):
   170	
   171	```
   172	SPREAD_FLOOR = 4.0   # snaps — a metronome must not flag on a wobble
   173	ABS_FLOOR    = 6     # snaps from his own median
   174	ABSENT_AT    = 2     # snaps this week
   175	ABSENT_MED   = 10    # ...against a prior median of at least this
   176	```
   177	
   178	A player is marked only when the week is past his own prior high or low **and** more
   179	than 1.5× his own interquartile spread from his own median **and** at least 6 snaps
   180	off that median. `≤2 snaps against a prior median of ≥10` is `absent` — an
   181	availability fact, drawn with a hollow mark, routed to the availability list. The
   182	threshold is drawn on every row so a coach can argue with it.

   186	## 4. What happens to each prototype script
   187	
   188	| Prototype file | Fate |
   189	|---|---|
   190	| `pull_snapshot.py` | **Splits.** HTTP half → `sources/cfbd.py`; DuckDB half → `sources/pff.py`; the orchestration → `snapshot.py`. Hardcoded constants → `resolve.py`. The `seasonType` filter moves in here (§1.3). |
   191	| `derive.py` | **Folds into `payload.py` and `metrics.py`.** Its separate `report_data.json` stage disappears — it exists only because the prototype grew in two sittings. Its `DIRECTION`/`LABEL` maps merge with `PATHS` in `build_payloads.py`, which already carries both. |
   192	| `build_payloads.py` | **The product.** `meta` block → `payload.build_meta()`. `band_rows`/`quantile`/`pctl_of` → `metrics.py`. Flag rule → `flags.py`. `GROUP`/`SIDE` position maps → `config.py`. |
   193	| `inject.py` | **Survives.** Becomes `render.py`. Keep the `</` escaping — it stops a payload string from closing the script block. |
   194	| `embed_fonts.py` | **Survives.** Becomes `fonts.py`. Already idempotent. Font cache moves to the repo's `fonts/` so the build works with no network. |
   195	| `patch_*.py`, `measure_*.html`, `shots/`, `m.html` | **Dropped.** One-off repair scripts from the prototype phase. |
   196	
   197	Salvaged copies of all of these live in `prototype_pipeline/` in this folder, with the
   198	JSON outputs in `prototype_pipeline/fixtures/`. They are the regression baseline (§6).

   202	## 5. How it runs
   203	
   204	```bash
   205	cd ~/DEV/Football/WeeklyCoachReport
   206	uv run coachreport run                    # resolves the week itself
   207	uv run coachreport run --week 6 --season 2025   # override, for a rebuild
   208	uv run coachreport run --offline          # rebuild from the cached snapshot, no API
   209	```
   210	
   211	Writes to `out/<season>/wk<NN>/`: two `.html`, two `.pdf`, both payloads, and the raw
   212	`snapshot.json`. Exits non-zero on any failed gate in §6 — a failed run must not leave
   213	a half-good PDF that looks finished.
   214	
   215	**Credentials.** `CFBD_API_KEY` is read at runtime from
   216	`~/DEV/CFB_Analytics_Lab/.env`; the PFF DuckDB at
   217	`~/DEV/Football/PFF_Data_Project/data/pff.db` is opened **read-only**. Values are never
   218	copied into code, docs, payloads or output. The PFF database is already backfilled and
   219	must not be rebuilt.
   220	
   221	**Phase 2 (not this season):** a launchd agent calling the same command Monday morning,
   222	plus a failure notification. The CLI is written so this needs no code change.

   226	## 6. Acceptance gates
   227	
   228	The build fails if any of these fail. They are the prototype's verified state, turned
   229	into tests.
   230	
   231	| # | Gate | How |
   232	|---|---|---|
   233	| G1 | Each edition renders exactly 5 pages at 8.50 × 11.00 in | PDF page count and page box, not eyeballing |
   234	| G2 | No sheet content exceeds one page | `scrollHeight` per `.sheet` at desktop width ≤ 1056 px |
   235	| G3 | No `undefined` / `NaN` in rendered sheet text | text scrape of the rendered DOM |
   236	| G4 | Renders identically with no network | headless Chrome with `--host-resolver-rules="MAP * 0.0.0.0"`; every expected family present in `document.fonts` with `status === "loaded"` (NOT `document.fonts.check()`, which returns true for fonts that do not exist) |
   237	| G5 | No font host referenced | `fonts.googleapis.com` / `fonts.gstatic.com` absent from output HTML |
   238	| G6 | Zero hardcoded weekly variables | grep `templates/` and `src/` (excluding `config.py` and `audit.py` itself, which holds the opponent list) for a 4-digit year, `Week \d`, and each Big-12 school name |
   239	| G7 | Golden payload regression | rebuilding 2025 week 6 from `tests/fixtures/snapshot.json` reproduces the saved `payload_a.json` / `payload_b.json` |
   240	| G8 | Both editions quote the same `box_snaps` | assert equality between payloads |
   241	| G9 | No postseason row survives | assert every week appears at most once in `season_tempo`, `epa_by_week`, `schedule` |
   242	| G10 | Voice check | no tier words (`elite`, `poor`, `bad`, `weak`, `underperform`) in generated player copy |
   243	
   244	G7 is the reason the fixtures were salvaged: it pins the whole derive layer against a
   245	week that was reviewed by a human.

   250	
   251	- **The fixes made after the final verdict pass were never re-reviewed.** Before the

   254	- **Page fill runs 35–95%.** The out-of-range sheet's height varies with how many

     1	# Product
     2	
     3	<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->
     4	
     5	## Platform
     6	
     7	web
     8	
     9	## Stack
    10	
    11	static HTML/CSS/JS, single self-contained file per edition, rendered to PDF via headless Chrome.
    12	Delegated: chosen because the deliverable is a printed page, the data is a small pre-computed
    13	JSON payload, and the file must survive being emailed and re-rendered without a build step.
    14	
    15	## Users
    16	
    17	Baylor Football, weekly during the season. Two editions of one report, same week, same data spine:
    18	
    19	- **Coaches edition** — head coach, coordinators, and position coaches. Read in the Monday
    20	  meeting room, on paper, in a short window, usually alongside film. They want what last
    21	  Saturday actually says and what next Saturday demands.
    22	- **Performance edition** — strength & conditioning and medical staff. Same week, same game,
    23	  but the question is exposure and workload, not tendencies. Drives training-week planning
    24	  and who gets watched.
    25	
    26	Author and owner: Applied Performance. The unit has published football analytics to this
    27	audience before (the 2024 `BU_Post_2024` EPA report), so the audience already accepts a
    28	report from this group.
    29	
    30	## Product Purpose
    31	
    32	One weekly report, produced Monday, that tells Baylor what the last game cost and what the
    33	next opponent will demand — in two editions cut for two audiences. Success is that it ships
    34	every game week without hand-editing, and that a coach acts on something in it.
    35	
    36	The predecessor failed on maintenance, not content: its source required hand-editing opponent
    37	names, team colors, logos and the next-opponent block every single week. Zero weekly hand-edits
    38	is a hard product requirement, not a nice-to-have.
    39	
    40	## Positioning
    41	
    42	Applied Performance is the only group at Baylor that holds both the football data (PFF grades
    43	and snap counts, CFBD play-by-play) and the athlete data (Catapult GPS, VALD force plates,
    44	Perch VBT). No neighboring group can truthfully produce both editions from one spine.
    45	
    46	## Capabilities and evidence
    47	
    48	Working, verified data sources — all real, none simulated:
    49	
    50	- **PFF** — local DuckDB at `Football/PFF_Data_Project/data/pff.db`; 175,559 game grades,
    51	  4.7M stat rows, 2023–2025. Carries per-player snap counts across all phases.
    52	- **CFBD API** — games, PPA/EPA, advanced box (success rate, explosiveness, line yards,
    53	  havoc, by-down splits), drives, plays, and in-game win probability.
    54	- Not yet joined into this report, but owned by the same unit: Catapult GPS
    55	  (`DataBase_GPS_Reporting`), VALD ForceDecks (`ForcePlate_DecisionSystem`), Perch VBT
    56	  (`PlayerProfiles_FB_03-28-2026`). A GPS-to-snap-count join is explicitly deferred.
    57	
    58	## Constraints
    59	
    60	- **Print is the primary medium.** The report is printed and handed out. Every fact must be
    61	  visible on the page; nothing may live only in a hover state. Fixed page sizes, page-break
    62	  control, and ink-safe color are requirements. Screen interaction may exist, but only as a
    63	  bonus on top of a page that is complete without it.
    64	- **Zero weekly hand-edits.** Opponent, next opponent, colors, and logos resolve from data.
    65	- Season window runs late August to mid-January; the report has no value outside a game week.
    66	- Athlete-level data carries FERPA obligations (see `SpeedProfiling/AGENTS.md`); the
    67	  performance edition names players and must be handled accordingly.
    68	- Reviewer preference on file (Kenny Boyd): no blame-implying rationale and no judgmental
    69	  tier labels. Workload bands and player flags must read as descriptive, never as a verdict
    70	  on the athlete.
    71	
    72	## Terminology
    73	
    74	EPA / PPA (expected points added per play), success rate, explosiveness, line yards, havoc
    75	rate, stuff rate, snap count, exposure, workload baseline. Power Four = SEC, Big Ten, Big 12,
    76	ACC — the comparison set for all percentiles.
    77	
    78	## Open decisions
    79	
    80	- Whether the two editions ship as two documents or one document with a split section.
    81	- Whether the GPS/force-plate/VBT join enters the performance edition this season or next.
################ DESIGN.md ################
     1	# Design — The Monitoring Chart
     2	
     3	<!-- impeccable:design-schema 1 -->
     4	
     5	Recorded from the built prototypes, not from intention. Two editions of one weekly
     6	report: `prototype_a_analytics.tpl.html` (coaches) and `prototype_b_demand.tpl.html`
     7	(performance). Both are printed handouts first and web pages second.
     8	
     9	## The idea the system encodes
    10	
    11	Read the team the way you read an athlete. Every measure is drawn against a reference
    12	range, the observed value is plotted on that range, and **only values outside the range
    13	are marked**. A quiet page is a real reading, not an empty one.
    14	
    15	This is what the system refuses: the sports-analytics dashboard. No KPI tiles, no card
    16	grid, no hero metric, no team-colour chrome.
    17	
    18	## Medium
    19	
    20	US Letter, portrait. Each `.sheet` is `8.5in × 11in` with `min-height: 11in`,
    21	`page-break-after: always`, and `@page { size: letter; margin: 0 }`. One sheet is one
    22	page — verify by PDF page count, never by eye. Content is measured with
    23	`scrollHeight` at desktop width; anything over 1056px spills a page.
    24	
    25	Two rules follow from print being primary:
    26	
    27	- **Nothing may live only in a hover state.** There are no tooltips. Every figure a
    28	  reader needs is set in type on the page.
    29	- **Narrow-screen overrides must be scoped `@media screen and (...)`.** An unscoped
    30	  `max-width: 9in` query fires during printing, because the page box is 8.5in wide, and
    31	  silently reflows the whole document into mobile layout.
    32	
    33	`html { -webkit-print-color-adjust: exact; print-color-adjust: exact }` keeps the paper,
    34	the row bands and the flagged grounds when someone prints from a browser with
    35	"Background graphics" off.
    36	
    37	## Colour
    38	
    39	| Token | Value | Role |
    40	|---|---|---|
    41	| `--desk` | `#c9ccc3` | the surface the sheets sit on, screen only |
    42	| `--paper` | `#f4f5f1` | sheet ground |
    43	| `--band` | `#e6ece3` | alternating row band |
    44	| `--ink` | `#15181a` | primary type |
    45	| `--ink-2` | `#454e4b` | prose, secondary figures |
    46	| `--ink-3` | `#5d6763` | 7.5pt labels, axis text, footnotes — 5.35:1 on paper |
    47	| `--rule` / `--rule-hair` | `#aab3ac` / `#c6cdc5` | table and box rules |
    48	| `--ref` | `#c3d0d4` | interquartile fill; outlined in `--ink-3` because the fill alone is under the 3:1 non-text floor |
    49	| `--obs` | `#2a6ea3` | the observed value, inside its range |
    50	| `--out` | `#b8433a` | **outside the reference range — this meaning only** |
    51	| `--neutral` | `#6b7a76` | derived marks that rank or net, never an alarm |
    52	
    53	Four inks. The discipline that matters: **`--out` means one thing.** A derived bar (an
    54	opponent's rank against the median, a net-EPA bar) uses `--neutral`, because an opponent's
    55	weakness is good news and painting it in the alarm colour says the opposite.
    56	
    57	`--obs` and `--out` were validated as a categorical pair against the paper surface
    58	(all-pairs CVD, chroma, lightness, contrast — all pass). Colour is never the only channel:
    59	every marked value also carries a drawn glyph and a worded reading.
    60	
    61	## Type
    62	
    63	- **Archivo Narrow** 600/700, uppercase, tracked — chart labels, headings, group tags.
    64	- **JetBrains Mono** 400/500/700, `tabular-nums` — every number, without exception.
    65	- **Source Sans 3** — the few sentences of prose; measure held under 72ch.
    66	
    67	Monospace is for measurement, not for atmosphere.
    68	
    69	All three are **self-hosted as inline `@font-face` data URIs** — no `<link>`, no font
    70	host, nothing to fetch. PRODUCT.md requires a file that survives being emailed and
    71	re-rendered, and a page whose type depends on a network round trip does not.
    72	
    73	Each family is a variable font, so every requested weight returns a byte-identical file.
    74	Embed each family **once** with `font-weight: 400 700`, not once per weight: 78 KB raw
    75	across all three instead of 189 KB. Regenerate with
    76	`prototype_pipeline/embed_fonts.py`, which is idempotent — it replaces the block it wrote
    77	last time. Verify by rendering with DNS blackholed
    78	(`--host-resolver-rules="MAP * 0.0.0.0"`) and confirming `document.fonts.check()` still
    79	returns true for all three.
    80	
    81	## Marks
    82	
    83	All marks are authored SVG paths in one stroke language — never glyph fonts, never emoji.
    84	
    85	| Mark | Meaning |
    86	|---|---|
    87	| filled circle, `--obs` | observed value, inside its reference range |
    88	| filled triangle up, `--out` | above the range |
    89	| filled triangle down, `--out` | below the range |
    90	| **hollow** triangle down, `--out` | did not feature — an availability fact, not a load one |
    91	| outlined circle | a scoring play on the win-probability trace |
    92	| dashed vertical rule, `--neutral` | the flag threshold, drawn so it can be argued with |
    93	| bar from a centre rule, `--neutral` | a derived offset (rank, net) — not a range judgement |
    94	
    95	The reference track is one component, reused everywhere: a whisker for the full range,
    96	a filled outlined box for the middle half, a rule at the median, and one mark for the
    97	observed value. On the coaches edition the axis is polarity-corrected so **further right
    98	is always better, including on defensive rows**. On the performance edition every player
    99	row shares one snap axis so exceedances are comparable between rows.
   100	
   101	## Honesty rules the charts must keep
   102	
   103	These are the failures this design was corrected for. They are cheap to reintroduce.
   104	
   105	1. **Never draw an axis annotation the data cannot support.** The win-probability feed
   106	   carries no clock field, so the trace has no quarter rules; its axis is labelled play
   107	   sequence.
   108	2. **One polarity per axis.** Measures that disagree about which direction is good do not
   109	   share a left-to-right axis. Plot their difference instead.
   110	3. **One spine per quantity.** Snap counts come from the advanced box in both editions,
   111	   from one shared payload field. Where a second source appears (the drive chart, the
   112	   play-by-play feed), the sheet says on the page why its totals differ and which to use.
   113	4. **A section header that implies completeness must be complete.** No `slice()` behind
   114	   a heading that says "players outside their range".
   115	5. **Never rescale an axis to swallow the value being judged.** It hides the size of the
   116	   exceedance and deforms the range it is judged against.
   117	6. **State magnitude, not just status.** "1 snap past the top of it" is a different fact
   118	   from "17 snaps under the bottom of it".
   119	
   120	## Zero weekly hand-edits
   121	
   122	The predecessor died of hand-editing. Every weekly variable is derived once in the
   123	payload builder into `meta` — opponent, next opponent and week, dates in both forms,
   124	`week_label`, `season_range`, `baseline_range`, result, home/away — and filled into
   125	slots at runtime. **No opponent name, date, week number or season label may be typed
   126	into markup.** The same rule covers computed claims: a sentence like "the widest gap of
   127	the season" is generated from the same rows that render beside it, never written down.
   128	
   129	Pagination follows the same rule. Rows per sheet are computed from page height, not
   130	set as a constant.
   131	
   132	## Voice
   133	
   134	Descriptive, never a verdict on a person. The performance edition says so on the sheet:
   135	*"This is a description of exposure, not a judgement of the athlete."* Bands are neutral
   136	("above his own range"), never tiered ("elite", "poor"). Low-exposure findings route to
   137	"worth confirming against the availability list" rather than implying a cause. This
   138	honours a standing reviewer preference: no blame-implying rationale, no judgemental tier
   139	labels.
   140	
   141	## What counts as out of range
   142	
   143	A threshold that fires on noise gets ignored, and then the real one gets ignored with it.
   144	The player rule takes the **stricter of two tests**, because each alone fails a real case:
   145	
   146	- the **observed range** alone flags one snap past a five-game maximum;
   147	- the **spread fence** alone flags a very consistent player for a game he has already
   148	  played, and misses a large jump by a volatile one.
   149	
   150	So a player is marked only when the week is past his own prior high or low **and** more
   151	than 1.5× his own interquartile spread from his own median **and** at least 6 snaps off
   152	that median. The spread is floored at 4 snaps so a metronome does not flag on a wobble.
   153	
   154	At the frozen constants the 6-snap floor is implied by the spread fence (1.5 × 4 = 6) and does not independently gate any case today; it exists as a ceiling so that a future change to `K` or `SPREAD_FLOOR` cannot authorize a flag under 6 snaps without deliberately revisiting this floor too.
   155	
   156	Two states, not one:
   157	
   158	| State | Rule | Mark |
   159	|---|---|---|
   160	| above / below | past both thresholds | filled triangle, `--out` |
   161	| did not feature | ≤2 snaps against a prior median of ≥10 | **hollow** triangle, `--out` |
   162	
   163	"Did not feature" is an availability fact, not a workload deviation, and the copy says so
   164	— it routes to the availability list rather than implying a training decision. Its mark is
   165	hollow so it never reads as the same kind of event.
   166	
   167	The trigger threshold is **drawn on every row** as a dashed rule, and the full rule is
   168	stated in prose on the sheet, so a coach can argue with it instead of trusting it. On the
   169	week this was built the rule marks 12 of the 51 players with an established baseline —
   170	2 above their own range, 2 below it, and 8 who did not feature. The previous plain
   171	min/max rule marked 15 of the same players, including one a single snap past a
   172	five-game best.
   173	
   174	## Known open work
   175	
   176	- Page fill runs 35–95%. The out-of-range findings get their own sheet because their
   177	  height varies with how many players flag; a quiet week leaves paper blank, and a heavy
   178	  week would otherwise overflow. Stability was chosen over density — a weekly automated
   179	  report cannot be re-laid-out by hand.
   180	- Narrow-viewport rendering clips wide tables and overlaps the sheet footer. Acceptable
   181	  while print is primary; it is not a screen product yet.
   182	- `--ref` (the interquartile fill) is under the 3:1 non-text floor on its own and depends
   183	  on its `--ink-3` outline to carry the shape. Fine in ink; worth revisiting if the fill
   184	  ever has to stand alone.

    24	## Architecture
    25	
    26	Four layers, one direction of flow:
    27	
    28	**fetch** (`sources/cfbd.py`, `sources/pff.py`) → **resolve** (`resolve.py`) → **derive** (`snapshot.py`, `metrics.py`, `flags.py`, `payload.py`) → **render** (`fonts.py`, `render.py`, `pdf.py`), with `cli.py` orchestrating and `audit.py` gating.
    29	
    30	Only the derive layer holds product logic, so it carries the heavy tests. The templates are the reviewed prototypes, moved not rewritten; they receive one JSON payload and are never hand-edited.
    31	
    32	### The requirement that shapes everything
    33	
    34	**Zero weekly hand-edits.** The predecessor report died of somebody typing the opponent, the week and the next game in every Monday. `resolve.py` derives all of it from the schedule; `payload.build_meta` turns it into the ~21 `meta` keys the sheets print. No opponent name, date, week number, season label or PFF team id may be a literal anywhere except `config.py`. Gate G6 (`audit.py`) enforces this across `templates/` and `src/`.
    35	
    36	### Invariants that are easy to break and hard to notice
    37	
    38	- **`seasonType == "regular"` is filtered exactly once**, in `sources/cfbd.py`. CFBD mixes a postseason row into season queries relabelled with a regular-season week number. Nothing downstream re-filters; adding a "defensive" second filter is a spec violation.
    39	- **One spine per quantity.** `box_snaps` and `meta` are each computed once and *the same object* is handed to both editions. Tests assert identity (`is`), not equality — two editions computing the same number separately is how they drifted apart the first time.
    40	- **Polarity is pre-corrected.** `metrics.band_rows` emits a `pctl` already flipped for `direction`, so further right is always better, including on defensive rows. The template never re-orients an axis. All 14 directions are pinned by test; a wrong-but-valid one silently inverts a printed chart.
    41	- **Deterministic ordering is a product requirement.** The report is printed and handed out, so identical input must yield an identical document. Every tie-capable `sorted()` in `payload.py` carries a secondary key, and `pff.py` queries carry `ORDER BY`. There is a test that shuffles and reverses the source rows and demands identical output.
    42	- **Flag constants are frozen** (`K=1.5`, `SPREAD_FLOOR=4.0`, `ABS_FLOOR=6`, `ABSENT_AT=2`, `ABSENT_MED=10`) and `DESIGN.md` documents each. Note `K * SPREAD_FLOOR == ABS_FLOOR` exactly, so **`ABS_FLOOR` is currently a no-op** — verified exhaustively over ~19.9M cases. It is kept as a ceiling in case `K` or `SPREAD_FLOOR` is ever lowered, and a test pins the relationship so a constant edit cannot pass unnoticed.
    43	- **"Did not feature" is an availability fact, not a workload deviation.** The `absent` check runs before high/low, and the copy says so. Voice is descriptive, never a verdict on a person — gate G10 bans tier words.
    44	
    45	### Environment traps, all verified in this repo
    46	
    47	- **`document.fonts.check()` is unusable in this Chrome build** — it returns `true` for fonts that do not exist, on a page with zero registered faces. A gate built on it passes on any input. `pdf.probe` walks the live `FontFaceSet` for `status === "loaded"` instead. Never reintroduce `check()`.
    48	- **`--evaluate-on-new-document` is a Puppeteer API, not a Chrome CLI flag.** Chrome ignores unknown flags silently, so a probe written that way returns nothing and G2/G3/G4 pass vacuously. `probe()` injects into a temp copy and reads `<title>` via `--dump-dom`, and **raises `ProbeFailed` rather than returning `{}`**.
    49	- **Headless Chrome's default viewport is 756px**, under the templates' `@media screen and (max-width: 9in)` breakpoint — measuring there measures the mobile layout while judging a printed page. `probe()` forces `--window-size=1280,1200`.
    50	- **`assert` is stripped under `-O`.** Load-bearing guards raise explicitly. A silently data-less sheet looks structurally fine and is blank where the numbers go.
    51	- **Always pass `encoding="utf-8"`** on template read/write. The templates carry the design's own glyphs (`▲ ● → · – —`); a stripped locale mangles a printed page rather than crashing.
    52	
    53	## Working on this codebase
    54	
    55	**The recurring defect here is tests that cannot fail** — nine instances across the twelve build tasks, including one in the offline-font gate itself. Before committing a test, ask concretely: *if I deleted the behavior this names, would this fail?* Prove it by breaking the behavior, showing the failure, then restoring. Watch for: `all()` over a possibly-empty list; `pytest.raises` without `match=`; asserting equality where identity is the requirement; a fixture whose values make the assertion hold either way.
    56	
    57	**The golden fixtures in `tests/fixtures/` are human-reviewed artifacts.** `expected_payload_a.json` / `expected_payload_b.json` pin the whole derive layer against a week a person checked. If a golden test fails, the difference is real — diff it, decide which side is right, and report. Never edit an expected payload to make a test pass.
    58	
    59	**The PFF DuckDB** (`~/DEV/Football/PFF_Data_Project/data/pff.db`) is a shared, already-backfilled 4.7M-row database owned by another project. Open read-only, never rebuild, never create files in that folder.
    60	
    61	**`out/` is git-ignored and stays that way** — it holds PDFs naming athletes with their snap counts, under the FERPA obligations `PRODUCT.md` describes. The two editions ship as two documents for the same reason: a single handout would put athlete workload data in every position coach's hands.

    34	### Exit codes
    35	
    36	| Code | Meaning |
    37	|---|---|
    38	| `0` | Clean. Every gate passed. |

    41	| `4` | The week is already built. Re-run with `--force`, or pass `--week`/`--offline`, either of which already counts as an explicit rebuild request. |
    42	
    43	A failed run still writes whatever it rendered before the gate check ran — the
    44	exit code, not the presence of a file, is what tells you whether it's usable.
