## Sport coverage — the honest baseline

The report exists to make involvement across sports visible. The roster of programs
comes from `DEV/AppliedUtilization/Applied_Performance_Utilization_Brief.md`
§"Recommendations by program" — 15 rows covering all 17 varsity programs. That document
is **prescriptive** (what each program *could* use), not a record of activity. Measured
against evidence, here is where things actually stand:

| | Programs |
|---|---|
| **Named in a project** | Football, Volleyball |
| **Named in the OKRs** | Football (via AJ), Volleyball (direct) — KR 1.1, and no others |
| **Appearing in meeting filenames** | Football (11), Baseball (5), Volleyball (3), Softball (2), Acro & Tumbling (2), Tennis (1), Women's Golf (1) |
| **Zero evidence anywhere** | Men's Basketball, Women's Basketball, Soccer, Men's/Women's XC & Track, Equestrian, Men's Golf, Men's/Women's Tennis as distinct programs |

Two findings worth sitting with before the first report goes out:

**Both basketball programs have zero recorded activity** — no meetings, no projects — yet
they carry detailed recommendations in the utilization brief. That is the largest single
gap between what the department says it offers and what it demonstrably delivers.

**Soccer is the brief's own "highest ROI now" pick** and its best-fit GPS use case, with
zero meetings and zero projects against it. If the ask driving this report is broader
involvement beyond football and basketball, soccer is the cheapest, best-documented
place to start — the case for it is already written in your own brief.

Also note: `RTP` (24 meetings) and `StaffMeet` (29) dwarf every individual sport in the
corpus, but no document lists them as served groups. Real work is happening in lanes
none of your planning documents describe, which is its own kind of invisibility.

Four projects carry `TBD` sports. If any of them serve a program outside football and
volleyball, that involvement is currently invisible in every system you have — which
means it is invisible to Chris and Kenny too.

## Summary

### Overview

- **Small Data Challenge:** Youth cricket scouting struggles with limited match data, typically 140-200 balls per player, making stats unreliable.  
- **Player Selection Dilemma:** Deciding between flashy but small-sample performance vs. consistent long-term stats requires multi-factor judgment beyond raw numbers.
