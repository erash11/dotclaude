    25	## Philosophy and decision principles
    26	
    27	- Applied Performance's stated mission is to "drive elite performance ... by leveraging science and technology through education and relationships," enabling the high-performance team to answer questions, ask better questions, and discover new ones — not to hand down answers unilaterally. **Explicit** [baylor-applied-performance-playbook.md p.6]
    28	- The department's four operating principles are Athlete Monitoring and Analysis, Athlete Evaluation and Return-to-Performance, Performance Team Collaboration, and Research and Development. **Explicit** [baylor-applied-performance-playbook.md p.6]
    29	- Institutional framing: "Prepare Champions for Life" across four pillars (academic, athletic, social, spiritual), operationalized through six "One Standard, One Accord" principles. **Explicit** [baylor-applied-performance-playbook.md pp.3-4]
    30	- Data collection is judged on cost versus insight: "Data collection has a cost in time, athlete compliance, and staff attention. When the insight stops justifying that cost, it's time to stop collecting." **Explicit** [2026-02-19-sportsmith-6otb-er.md]
    31	- A test can be scientifically valid and still be the wrong choice operationally: "The lesson wasn't about the test. It was about the infrastructure required to make a test useful ... even a valid test stops being valuable" without staff ownership and consistent collection. **Explicit** [2026-02-20-sportsmith-6otb-nordbord-er.md]
    32	- General rule for any test or technology, stated as a standing rule rather than a fresh call each time. Eric (interview 2026-09-28, Q3): "If it doesn't answer a question anyone has, or a direct need, then it doesn't have a place." **Explicit (interview 2026-09-28, Q3)**
    33	- The coach's eye and objective data work in sequence: the eye flags, data investigates. Eric (interview 2026-09-28, Q6): "There is a place for the coach's eye: when something looks off, it can direct you where to look and gather data to investigate further." **Explicit (interview 2026-09-28, Q6)**
    34	- "Inconsistent data from a good test is still bad data" — a test's validity doesn't rescue it if it can't be collected consistently at scale. **Explicit** [2026-02-20-sportsmith-6otb-nordbord-er.md]
    35	- Reject ownership language ("my team," "my athletes"): the athlete's development is shared across every department that touches them, not owned by any one coach. **Inferred (transcript, speaker unconfirmed)** [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md p.5, "we got to get away from like the me and my"]
    36	- Attribution in practice: outside frameworks are absorbed into the team's own way of working rather than credited to one person. Eric (interview 2026-09-28, Q27): "We fold it into the mantra of 'this is how we do our business.'" **Explicit (interview 2026-09-28, Q27)** Inference: this is how Eric applies the "we/our" bullet above, so that bullet reads as practice, though the transcript line itself is still unclaimed.
    37	- The "five expectations" culture rules (Golden Rule, Be Disciplined, Be Relentless, Communicate, No Excuses) were presented in the course as absolute [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md pp.1-2]. Eric (interview 2026-09-28, Q26): "These are more a relic of another coaching era that has held over. I would say none [no deliberate exception], but they are rarely mentioned around here any more." **Legacy (interview 2026-09-28, Q26)**
    38	- Injury risk can be reduced, not eliminated, through sound programming, progression, and collaboration with Athletic Medicine — an explicit refusal to promise injury prevention. **Inferred (transcript)** [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md p.4]
    39	- Be skeptical of social-media-driven training trends: a viral clip is "just a brief snapshot," stripped of the context that would tell you whether it's sound. **Inferred (transcript)** [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md p.3]
    40	- Recovery has to be earned by training hard first; a culture of fear grew out of early GPS/load-monitoring work that pushed teams toward under-training in the name of "recovery." **Inferred (transcript)** [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md pp.9-10]
    41	- "Knowledge from experience" as a stated operating value: safety first, keep things simple, teach basic movement skills and basic lifts before anything advanced, treat strength as the base, and require bodyweight competency before loaded work. **Inferred (transcript + Week 1 slides)** [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md pp.4-5; week1-lecture.md p.12]

    54	## Testing and assessment
    55	
    56	- Assessment is continuous, not a single test day: start with movement quality, injury history, and training background, then observe daily via warm-ups and practice. **Inferred (transcript)** [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md pp.4-5]
    57	- Testing protocol has to be exactly repeatable over time ("how you do it in January needs to be the same as how you do it in April") or the resulting data cannot be trusted for comparison. **Inferred (transcript)** [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md p.6]
    58	- Favor a small number of valid, reliable tests mapped to clear KPI categories over an exhaustive battery — illustrated with a simple baseball profile (bodyweight, 60-yard dash, vertical jump, broad jump, back squat, push-up max). **Inferred (transcript + Week 1 slides)** [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md p.6; week1-lecture.md p.16]
    59	- Weekly tracking is preferred over infrequent (e.g., once-a-phase) testing, specifically to catch plateaus or declines early enough to adjust programming. **Inferred (transcript)** [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md p.7] (See the cadence bullet below: there is no department-wide cadence.)
    60	- There is no common testing cadence across sports; each sport keeps its own schedule. Eric (interview 2026-09-28, Q9): "We currently don't have a consistent cadence across all sports. Individual sports have their own unique schedules." **Explicit (interview 2026-09-28, Q9)**
    61	- No sport performs true 1RM testing. Maxes are projected from observation plus the athlete's history. Eric (interview 2026-09-28, Q10): "things are ballparked based on other information we're grabbing on the athlete: watching them move in real time, then using historical context to make future programming decisions around projected 1RMs." **Explicit (interview 2026-09-28, Q10)**
    62	- Change of direction is not tested in most sports. Eric (interview 2026-09-28, Q13): "If the data is captured, it's on an individual sport basis and that information lives with that coach." **Explicit (interview 2026-09-28, Q13)** The RTP templates still gate on COD and FMS, run by Athletic Medicine or the sport coach (Tension 18, resolved 2026-10-02).
    63	- The Assessment Manual V12 is not current. A new, more robust manual is in development and will incorporate the NCAA technology and sport-science recommendations. **Explicit (interview 2026-09-28, Q8)** Every bullet below sourced to `ap-assessment-manual-draft-v12.md` is therefore Legacy.
    64	- Preparedness (the athlete's underlying physical capability, built over a long period) is explicitly distinguished from readiness (day-to-day fluctuation) — both are tracked, but they answer different questions. **Inferred (transcript)** [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md p.7]
    65	- Reassessment timing is governed by three factors: proximity to competition, the expected magnitude of change for that training phase and athlete level, and current recovery status: "do not reassess an athlete if there is known fatigue." **Legacy (interview 2026-09-28, Q8)**; was Inferred (author uncertain) [ap-assessment-manual-draft-v12.md, "Reassessment"]
    66	- 1RM testing is reserved for intermediate/advanced athletes with real exposure to near-maximal loads; novices and most women's-sport testing default to repetition-max protocols, which are described as better tolerated neurologically, psychologically, and physically for smaller-framed lifters. **Legacy (interview 2026-09-28, Q10)**: no sport tests true 1RM now; was Inferred (transcript + Week 1 slides) [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md p.18; week1-lecture.md p.39]
    67	- Velocity-based 1RM estimation (linear-displacement transducer, ~10% load increments to 85-90% or a target mean velocity) is preferred over live 1RM attempts to avoid the fatigue and injury risk of true maxing, with published target mean velocities at 1RM (squat 0.3 m/s, bench 0.2 m/s, power hang snatch 1.0 m/s, power hang clean 0.8 m/s). **Legacy (interview 2026-09-28, Q8, Q10)**; was Inferred (author uncertain) [ap-assessment-manual-draft-v12.md] Inference: Q10 describes projection from observation and history and does not mention a velocity protocol.
    68	- The Baylor Sport Assessment Table (V4) assigns a specific battery per sport (movement screen, anthropometrics, CMJ, broad jump, weighted throw, upper/lower strength estimation, strength endurance, aerobic fitness, repeat sprint ability, linear sprint, change of direction) rather than testing every sport the same way. **Legacy (interview 2026-09-28, Q9)**; was Inferred. Eric (interview 2026-09-28, Q9): "That assessment schedule was never used." Inference: the table was never practice, not just outdated. [update-baylor-sport-assessment-table-v4.md]
    69	- The current data dictionary is organized by vendor (Catapult, Perch, ForceDecks, Polar), not by sport, which Eric's own gap analysis flags as a real usability problem for staff and a priority to fix with sport-specific, threshold-contextualized dictionaries. **Explicit** [performance-playbook-development-plan.md 3.2.3, Phase 2]

    71	## Force plate and neuromuscular monitoring
    72	
    73	- Force plates (CMJ testing) were already the department's longitudinal performance-tracking tool well before the 2022-23 wellness-survey experiment, and remained in place after that experiment ended. **Explicit** [2026-02-19-sportsmith-6otb-er.md]
    74	- Modified reactive strength index (mRSI) from daily CMJ testing was deliberately chosen as the daily athlete-readiness marker in place of subjective wellness questionnaires, because it is "objective, consistent, and actionable in a way the wellness surveys never quite were." **Explicit** [2026-02-19-sportsmith-6otb-er.md]
    75	- The mRSI decision read happens on Wednesdays, against individualized thresholds, not a single cut-off or position-group thresholds. Eric (interview 2026-09-28, Q2): "Wednesdays, the thresholds should be individualized." Individualized against: Eric (interview 2026-09-28, Q2a): "Percent change from baseline or a rolling average. We've used standard deviations and SWC to diagnose signal versus noise." **Explicit (interview 2026-09-28, Q2, Q2a)** Inference: "should be" may describe the intended rule rather than a fully running one; no numeric threshold was given. See Tension 12 (daily testing versus a Wednesday read), resolved 2026-10-02: daily jumps build the baseline and SWC, Wednesday is the decision read.
    76	- The dynamic strength index (DSI: CMJ against isometric mid-thigh pull peak force) chooses between plyometric and max-strength emphasis. Eric (interview 2026-09-28, Q21): "we incorporate the countermovement jump and the isometric mid-thigh pull to get a dynamic strength index, to determine whether we need more plyometric training or more max-strength training." **Explicit (interview 2026-09-28, Q21)** This is not the SJ-CMJ gap the question asked about. Evidence check done 2026-09-29 (`docs/synthesis/_work/dsi-evidence-check.md`): cite it as Eric's practice, tagged [contested; the ratio tracks IMTP strength; one controlled trial (Pleša 2024, basketball, split at 0.90) supported only the strength arm; no football data]. See Tension 17.
    77	- The standard CMJ family run on ForceDecks: hands-free squat, squat jump (quarter-squat start, 3s hold), countermovement jump (x3), hop test / repeated CMJ (10 reps total, n=5 pairs), and single-leg CMJ (x3 per leg) — each with a scripted weigh-in, stabilization, and rep protocol. **Inferred (protocol; author uncertain)** [revised-force-plate-testing-v3.md]
    78	- The isometric seated calf raise is run on the ForceDecks "Mid-Thigh Pull" setting: 90-degree ankle/knee position, belt pre-tensioned to a fingertip's gap, 3 maximal 6-second contractions with 15 seconds' rest between reps. **Inferred (protocol)** [protocol-iso-seated-calf-raise.md; revised-force-plate-testing-v3.md]
    79	- Metric selection for force-plate reporting follows a disciplined framework: pick 1-2 output metrics (the obvious performance result, e.g. jump height), 1-3 driver metrics (what produces the output, e.g. braking/propulsive force), 1-3 strategy metrics (how the athlete achieved it, e.g. phase durations), optional symmetry metrics (L/R differences), and add "fluffy" metrics (peak power, average power, RFD) only with a specific, defensible rationale. **Adopted practice, framework not Eric's own** [hd-odsf-metrics.md — origin: Hawkin Dynamics]
    80	- NordBord hamstring testing was retired for roster-wide football monitoring (after starting January 2023) because a 25-30 minute per-group session could not be sustained across 120+ athletes without large, uncontrolled gaps in the data, and staff turnover ended the coordination needed to run it at scale. The decision rule stated: keep a demanding test only where you have "smaller numbers, clear clinical purpose, dedicated oversight" (now used for targeted return-to-play groups paired with joint-specific isometric testing); drop it where you can't guarantee consistent, comparable collection. **Explicit** [2026-02-20-sportsmith-6otb-nordbord-er.md]

    91	## Speed and sprint
    92	
    93	- Linear sprint testing uses a 2-point start for team-sport athletes (for reliability), athlete self-selected start timing, at least two maximal-effort reps over 30-40 meters. **Legacy (interview 2026-09-28, Q8)**; was Inferred (author uncertain) [ap-assessment-manual-draft-v12.md]
    94	- Split times are interpreted by physical quality, not just total time: 0-10m is read as an acceleration score (reflecting maximal strength/power/slow stretch-shortening-cycle development), 30-40m as a speed score (reflecting sprint-specific/fast stretch-shortening-cycle development), and 40m peak velocity is derived from the 30-40m split divided by 10. **Legacy (interview 2026-09-28, Q8)**; was Inferred (author uncertain) [ap-assessment-manual-draft-v12.md]
    95	- SmartSpeed timing gates are the standard tool for sprint, agility, and reaction-time testing across the department. **Explicit** [baylor-applied-performance-playbook.md p.7]
    96	- Speed is taught as the quality with the shortest ground-contact-time demand (~0.08s male, ~0.10s female at max velocity) and is described as harder to train and more genetically constrained than strength or power, but as the biggest separator between good and great athletes at the elite level. **Inferred (transcript)** [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md p.11]
    97	- Issurin's residual training effects (speed fades fastest, 5 ± 3 days; Block Periodization PDF p. 35, as cited in `docs/synthesis/peaking-and-taper-in-season-football.md`) drive speed programming. Eric (interview 2026-09-28, Q16): "I believe Issurin. It drives our speed programming in the off-season and what drives sprinting recommendations in season." **Explicit (interview 2026-09-28, Q16)** See the residual-effects bullets under Conditioning and Periodization, and Tension 11.

    99	## Strength and power
   100	
   101	- The Vermeil hierarchy of athletic development (work capacity, then strength, then power, then speed, then sport skill, each level a required foundation for the next) is repeatedly described as the department's programming "compass." **Explicit (interview 2026-09-28, Q1)**; was Inferred (transcript + shared course material) [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md pp.9-10; week1-lecture.md pp.23,26]
   102	- Exercise selection is organized on a spectrum from general preparation through specific preparation, specific development, to competitive exercises (Bondarchuk), replacing the loose and often-misapplied idea of "sport specific." **Inferred (transcript + slides)** [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md pp.13-16; week1-lecture.md pp.32-36]
   103	- The Principle of Dynamic Correspondence (Verkhoshansky), matching amplitude/direction of movement, region of force production, dynamics of effort, rate/timing of force production, and regime of muscular work to the sport skill, governs whether an exercise actually transfers, rather than surface resemblance to the sport. **Explicit (interview 2026-09-28, Q1)**; was Inferred (transcript + slides) [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md p.14; week1-lecture.md p.34]
   104	- Training time is deliberately weighted toward high motor-unit-recruitment core/ballistic movements (squats, cleans, jumps, throws, sprints); single-joint assistance work is kept to a small share of total volume. **Inferred (transcript + slides)** [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md p.13; week1-lecture.md p.31]
   105	- Off-season strength work prioritizes relative strength and technique first; already-strong athletes shift more time toward strength-speed/speed-strength work. In-season, the stated rule is "maintain intensity, reduce volume" rather than trying to build strength while managing a full competition schedule. **Inferred (transcript + shared slides)** [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md pp.20-21; hhpr-5355-class-8-2026.md p.21] Not claimed in the interview. The in-season dose itself is unknown: Eric (interview 2026-09-28, Q18): "I don't have the specific sets and reps."
   106	- Undulating/non-linear periodization (Parker/Miller/Panariello), waving volume week to week (e.g., medium-high, medium, high, low over four weeks) is stated as the personally preferred model over strict linear progression, specifically to avoid staleness while still building tolerance for higher loads. **Explicit (interview 2026-09-28, Q1)**; was Inferred (transcript, speaker unconfirmed but first-person: "For me personally, this is a more preferential model to use") [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md pp.24-26]
   107	- Prilepin's chart (total reps and reps-per-set by %1RM) is the default guide for daily rep/set assignment within a training block, used consistently across strength and power teaching. **Inferred/Explicit (shared team material)** [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md p.19; hhpr-5355-class-8-2026.md p.15] Inference: with no true 1RM testing (Q10), any %1RM prescription rests on projected maxes. Kept on that explicit basis (Tension 16, resolved 2026-10-02).
   108	- Strength's residual training effect is treated as roughly 30 days; a minimal retention dose is still trained within that window, even during lower-strength-emphasis blocks. **Inferred (transcript)** [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md pp.24-25] Inference: consistent with Eric siding with Issurin (Q16), whose strength residual is 30 ± 5 days, though Q16 was about speed and he did not claim this bullet.
   109	- Tracy Fober's maxim ("use a barbell like a chisel, not a sledgehammer") is adopted as a guiding standard: prioritize movement quality and confidence-through-competence over chasing hypertrophy or 1RM numbers, especially early in a training cycle. **Inferred (transcript + slides)** [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md p.21; week1-lecture.md p.46]

   111	## Conditioning and energy systems
   112	
   113	- Conditioning is defined two ways: (1) how well an athlete can meet the energy requirements of their specific sport/activity, and (2) how well they sustain strength, speed, and power output across a full competition. **Explicit** [week3-lecture.md p.22]
   114	- All three energy systems (alactic ~10-12s highest power, glycolytic/anaerobic-lactic ~60-90s medium capacity, aerobic hours-long lowest power/highest capacity) are always active simultaneously; programming targets the adaptation, not the drill label. **Explicit** [week3-lecture.md p.19; class-4-2026.md p.15]
   115	- A sport cannot maximize all three energy systems at once — priority has to be picked based on what the competition actually demands (e.g., for football: prepared for fall camp first, then the first game). **Explicit** [class-4-2026.md p.31]
   116	- For football specifically, the stated priority stack is: Goal 1, alactic and aerobic capacity/power for fall-camp demands; Goal 2, the same two systems for game demands; glycolytic conditioning is treated as a "worst case scenario" to be absorbed incidentally through camp and practice rather than programmed as a primary target. **Explicit** [class-4-2026.md pp.34, 39]; claimed again in the interview, **Explicit (interview 2026-09-28, Q1)**
