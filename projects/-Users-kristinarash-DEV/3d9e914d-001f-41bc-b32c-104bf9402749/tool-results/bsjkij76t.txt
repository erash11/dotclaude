##### MISSION.md #####
# Mission

Fluency in applying data-science techniques to football data — specifically the
methods in *Football Analytics with Python and R* (Eric A. Eager & Richard A.
Erickson, O'Reilly, 2023), transposed from the book's NFL/nflfastR data onto
college football data from the CollegeFootballData API.

## What fluency means here

Not "I ran the notebook." Fluency is: given a football question, choose an
appropriate method, fit it, and explain both what it says and where it breaks.

Each lesson ends in a drill completed unaided. The drill is the evidence.

## Scope

**In:** over-expected metrics (CPOE/RYOE), GLMs (logistic + Poisson), stability
analysis, clustering + PCA, tree ensembles, Bayesian hierarchical models.

**Out:** beating the betting market, Baylor coaching deliverables, and any change
to `~/DEV/CFB_Victory_Model` (which has a hard weekly cron dependency).

Bayesian methods are **not** in the book. Lesson 0007 is a deliberate extension
past the source material and is the steepest part of the curriculum.

## Why college data rather than the book's NFL data

The methods port directly — CFBD's `/plays` schema maps nearly one-to-one onto
nflfastR, and CFBD ships `ppa` per play where nflfastR ships `epa`. But college
football has a far wider talent spread (136 FBS teams plus FCS opponents), which
makes partial pooling genuinely more interesting here than in the NFL.

## Filter to FBS before analyzing

`/plays` ships FCS (and lower-division) games mixed in with FBS. CFBD's own
play coverage changes shape at the 2022 season boundary — FBS-only through
2021, full FCS coverage added from 2022 on — so any year-over-year analysis
run on unfiltered plays across that boundary silently compares a ~217-team
population against a ~300-team one and produces confident, wrong answers.
Always filter before analyzing:

```python
from cfblab.io import load_plays, load_victory_model_csv
from cfblab.filters import attach_classification, fbs_only

plays = load_plays("data/raw/plays")
games = load_victory_model_csv("games_*_*.csv")
fbs = fbs_only(attach_classification(plays, games))
```

`load_victory_model_csv` reads the `games_*_*.csv` files already on disk at
`~/DEV/CFB_Victory_Model/data/cfbd/` — zero API calls.

## FBS filtering — required before cross-2022 analysis

`/plays` ships FCS games alongside FBS, and CFBD's own play coverage changes
shape at the 2022 boundary: FBS-only through 2021, full FCS coverage added
from 2022 on. Distinct games per season in the raw pull are 887 | 568 | 887 |
1459 | 1477 | 1610 | 1659 (2019-2025) — that jump at 2022 is coverage, not a
real change in how many FBS games were played. Any year-over-year analysis
spanning 2022 that skips filtering compares a ~217-team population against a
~300-team one.

Filter with `attach_classification` + `fbs_only` before analyzing anything
that crosses that boundary:

```python
from cfblab.io import load_plays, load_victory_model_csv
from cfblab.filters import attach_classification, fbs_only

plays = load_plays("data/raw/plays")
games = load_victory_model_csv("games_*_*.csv")
fbs = fbs_only(attach_classification(plays, games))
```

`games_*_*.csv` (15 files, already on disk from `CFB_Victory_Model`, zero API
cost) carries `id`, `homeClassification`, `awayClassification` with zero
duplicate ids as of 2026-08-13. The merge uses `validate="m:1"` so a future
re-pull or wider glob that introduces duplicates raises `MergeError` instead
of silently multiplying play rows.

## Postseason coverage — week 1 only

`pull_years` iterates postseason weeks 1..MAX_POSTSEASON_WEEKS (3) and stops
at the first empty response. **CFBD's postseason weeks are non-contiguous:
1, then 11-15 (FCS playoff rounds), then 20 (FCS title game).** Week 2 for
every pulled season returns empty (no FBS bowl games are scheduled that
week), so the loop terminates immediately after week 1 and weeks 11-20 are
never fetched.

This was verified 2026-08-13 as a deliberate non-backfill: 127 postseason
games (all FCS/lower-division) plus 4 at 2019 regular week 16 were never
pulled. Of 5,252 completed FBS-vs-FBS games 2019-2025, **exactly one** is
absent — a CFBD data gap, not a puller defect. Backfilling would cost ~42
additional calls for zero FBS-relevant games and is intentionally not done.

**Postseason coverage is week 1 (FBS bowls + CFP) only.** FCS playoff rounds
(weeks 11-20) are deliberately not pulled. Zero FBS-vs-FBS games are
affected.

## Rulings

**Ruling 1: `pull_years` must stamp `season`, `week`, and `seasonType` before writing Parquet.**

The `/plays` response schema — verified by live probe 2026-08-13 — contains no
`season`, `week`, or `seasonType` field. Records carry only `gameId, driveId, id,
driveNumber, playNumber, offense, ..., ppa, wallclock`. But three downstream tasks
depend on `season` existing on the stored data:

- T4 `add_covid_flag(plays, season_col="season")`
- T5 `load_plays` / `query` (tests seed a `season` column)
- T8 `SELECT season, COUNT(*) FROM plays GROUP BY season` — the primary assertion

As written, Task 3 would produce Parquet that Tasks 4, 5, and 8 cannot use, and the
failure would surface only at Task 8 after the live pull had already spent ~125 calls.

Decision: Task 3's `pull_years` assigns `season`, `week`, and `seasonType` onto the
normalized DataFrame before `.to_parquet()`. Carried into the Task 3 dispatch, with a
matching test.

Cost if wrong: negligible — three redundant columns on the raw Parquet. Cost of not
ruling: a wasted 125-call pull against a monthly budget that does not reset until Sept 1.
