Eligible studies were randomised or non-randomised comparative studies in healthy adults aged 18 to 40 with pre-post outcomes and an exercise intervention 1 to 12 h before testing. Studies using nutrition, supplements, heat or cold priming, stretching, massage, vibration or altitude were excluded. Two reviewers screened; PEDro scores were assigned, and studies were grouped into resistance training, cycling, running and other interventions.",
  "findings": "Twenty-nine studies were included. Resistance training with heavy loads at low volume increased strength and power after a 4 to 6 h recovery, with limited improvement after 1 to 3 h or 6 to 12 h. Running-based sprint priming improved subsequent sprint and repeat-sprint performance after 5 to 6 h. Cycling improved counter-movement jump height in one study only. No significant differences were reported after endurance running or cycling. Hormone and blood lactate responses were mixed, and in one study (Saez de Villareal et al.) loaded countermovement jump height improved only after a loaded countermovement jump protocol.",
