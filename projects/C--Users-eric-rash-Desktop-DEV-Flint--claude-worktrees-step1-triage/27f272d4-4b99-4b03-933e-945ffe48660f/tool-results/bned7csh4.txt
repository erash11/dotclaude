## Philosophy and decision principles

- Applied Performance's stated mission is to "drive elite performance ... by leveraging science and technology through education and relationships," enabling the high-performance team to answer questions, ask better questions, and discover new ones — not to hand down answers unilaterally. **Explicit** [baylor-applied-performance-playbook.md p.6]
- The department's four operating principles are Athlete Monitoring and Analysis, Athlete Evaluation and Return-to-Performance, Performance Team Collaboration, and Research and Development. **Explicit** [baylor-applied-performance-playbook.md p.6]
- Institutional framing: "Prepare Champions for Life" across four pillars (academic, athletic, social, spiritual), operationalized through six "One Standard, One Accord" principles. **Explicit** [baylor-applied-performance-playbook.md pp.3-4]
- Data collection is judged on cost versus insight: "Data collection has a cost in time, athlete compliance, and staff attention. When the insight stops justifying that cost, it's time to stop collecting." **Explicit** [2026-02-19-sportsmith-6otb-er.md]
- A test can be scientifically valid and still be the wrong choice operationally: "The lesson wasn't about the test. It was about the infrastructure required to make a test useful ... even a valid test stops being valuable" without staff ownership and consistent collection. **Explicit** [2026-02-20-sportsmith-6otb-nordbord-er.md]
- "Inconsistent data from a good test is still bad data" — a test's validity doesn't rescue it if it can't be collected consistently at scale. **Explicit** [2026-02-20-sportsmith-6otb-nordbord-er.md]
- Reject ownership language ("my team," "my athletes"): the athlete's development is shared across every department that touches them, not owned by any one coach. **Inferred (transcript, speaker unconfirmed)** [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md p.5, "we got to get away from like the me and my"]
- Injury risk can be reduced, not eliminated, through sound programming, progression, and collaboration with Athletic Medicine — an explicit refusal to promise injury prevention. **Inferred (transcript)** [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md p.4]
- Be skeptical of social-media-driven training trends: a viral clip is "just a brief snapshot," stripped of the context that would tell you whether it's sound. **Inferred (transcript)** [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md p.3]
- Recovery has to be earned by training hard first; a culture of fear grew out of early GPS/load-monitoring work that pushed teams toward under-training in the name of "recovery." **Inferred (transcript)** [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md pp.9-10]
- "Knowledge from experience" as a stated operating value: safety first, keep things simple, teach basic movement skills and basic lifts before anything advanced, treat strength as the base, and require bodyweight competency before loaded work. **Inferred (transcript + Week 1 slides)** [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md pp.4-5; week1-lecture.md p.12]

## Department model and roles

- Applied Performance operates inside the Baylor Athletics Health & Wellness department, alongside (not above or below) Athletics Performance, Athletic Medicine, Performance Nutrition, and Mental Health Services. **Explicit** [baylor-applied-performance-playbook.md pp.5-6]
- The athlete is described as the hub of a wheel, with every support function (academics, Athletic Medicine, nutrition, mental health, Applied Performance) as a spoke — no single discipline owns the athlete's development. **Inferred (transcript)** [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md p.5]
- An Athletic Performance coach is framed in explicit contrast to a narrower "Strength Coach" identity: "we/our" and holistic mind-body-spirit development versus "me/my" and just lifting/running. **Inferred (transcript + Week 1 slides)** [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md p.5; week1-lecture.md p.13]
- The current Baylor Applied Performance Operations Guide is judged, by Eric's own gap analysis, as missing: an intern/GA curriculum, sport-specific data dictionaries, daily operational checklists, Baylor-specific (not generic-template) technology workflows, and a guiding-principles/policy framework for staff and interns. **Explicit** [performance-playbook-development-plan.md Section 1, 3.2]
- A three-phase roadmap (Foundation, weeks 1-4; Operational Depth, weeks 5-10; Education and Culture, weeks 11-16+) is the stated plan to close those gaps, ending with an annual Playbook review cycle each May. **Explicit** [performance-playbook-development-plan.md Section 4]
- Some assessments coaches already run inside practice should not be duplicated; use that existing data to triangulate rather than re-testing, and set objective criteria for coach-mandated tests when none exist. **Inferred (author uncertain — see Testing tensions)** [ap-assessment-manual-draft-v12.md, "Team Cultures and Assessments"]

## Testing and assessment

- Assessment is continuous, not a single test day: start with movement quality, injury history, and training background, then observe daily via warm-ups and practice. **Inferred (transcript)** [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md pp.4-5]
- Testing protocol has to be exactly repeatable over time ("how you do it in January needs to be the same as how you do it in April") or the resulting data cannot be trusted for comparison. **Inferred (transcript)** [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md p.6]
- Favor a small number of valid, reliable tests mapped to clear KPI categories over an exhaustive battery — illustrated with a simple baseball profile (bodyweight, 60-yard dash, vertical jump, broad jump, back squat, push-up max). **Inferred (transcript + Week 1 slides)** [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md p.6; week1-lecture.md p.16]
- Weekly tracking is preferred over infrequent (e.g., once-a-phase) testing, specifically to catch plateaus or declines early enough to adjust programming. **Inferred (transcript)** [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md p.7]
- Preparedness (the athlete's underlying physical capability, built over a long period) is explicitly distinguished from readiness (day-to-day fluctuation) — both are tracked, but they answer different questions. **Inferred (transcript)** [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md p.7]
- Reassessment timing is governed by three factors: proximity to competition, the expected magnitude of change for that training phase and athlete level, and current recovery status — "do not reassess an athlete if there is known fatigue." **Inferred (author uncertain)** [ap-assessment-manual-draft-v12.md, "Reassessment"]
- 1RM testing is reserved for intermediate/advanced athletes with real exposure to near-maximal loads; novices and most women's-sport testing default to repetition-max protocols, which are described as better tolerated neurologically, psychologically, and physically for smaller-framed lifters. **Inferred (transcript + Week 1 slides)** [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md p.18; week1-lecture.md p.39]
- Velocity-based 1RM estimation (linear-displacement transducer, ~10% load increments to 85-90% or a target mean velocity) is preferred over live 1RM attempts to avoid the fatigue and injury risk of true maxing, with published target mean velocities at 1RM (squat 0.3 m/s, bench 0.2 m/s, power hang snatch 1.0 m/s, power hang clean 0.8 m/s). **Inferred (author uncertain — legacy protocol, see Tensions)** [ap-assessment-manual-draft-v12.md]
- The Baylor Sport Assessment Table (V4) assigns a specific battery per sport (movement screen, anthropometrics, CMJ, broad jump, weighted throw, upper/lower strength estimation, strength endurance, aerobic fitness, repeat sprint ability, linear sprint, change of direction) rather than testing every sport the same way. **Inferred** [update-baylor-sport-assessment-table-v4.md]
- The current data dictionary is organized by vendor (Catapult, Perch, ForceDecks, Polar), not by sport, which Eric's own gap analysis flags as a real usability problem for staff and a priority to fix with sport-specific, threshold-contextualized dictionaries. **Explicit** [performance-playbook-development-plan.md 3.2.3, Phase 2]

## Force plate and neuromuscular monitoring

- Force plates (CMJ testing) were already the department's longitudinal performance-tracking tool well before the 2022-23 wellness-survey experiment, and remained in place after that experiment ended. **Explicit** [2026-02-19-sportsmith-6otb-er.md]
- Modified reactive strength index (mRSI) from daily CMJ testing was deliberately chosen as the daily athlete-readiness marker in place of subjective wellness questionnaires, because it is "objective, consistent, and actionable in a way the wellness surveys never quite were." **Explicit** [2026-02-19-sportsmith-6otb-er.md]
- The standard CMJ family run on ForceDecks: hands-free squat, squat jump (quarter-squat start, 3s hold), countermovement jump (x3), hop test / repeated CMJ (10 reps total, n=5 pairs), and single-leg CMJ (x3 per leg) — each with a scripted weigh-in, stabilization, and rep protocol. **Inferred (protocol; author uncertain)** [revised-force-plate-testing-v3.md]
- The isometric seated calf raise is run on the ForceDecks "Mid-Thigh Pull" setting: 90-degree ankle/knee position, belt pre-tensioned to a fingertip's gap, 3 maximal 6-second contractions with 15 seconds' rest between reps. **Inferred (protocol)** [protocol-iso-seated-calf-raise.md; revised-force-plate-testing-v3.md]
- Metric selection for force-plate reporting follows a disciplined framework: pick 1-2 output metrics (the obvious performance result, e.g. jump height), 1-3 driver metrics (what produces the output, e.g. braking/propulsive force), 1-3 strategy metrics (how the athlete achieved it, e.g. phase durations), optional symmetry metrics (L/R differences), and add "fluffy" metrics (peak power, average power, RFD) only with a specific, defensible rationale. **Adopted practice, framework not Eric's own** [hd-odsf-metrics.md — origin: Hawkin Dynamics]
- NordBord hamstring testing was retired for roster-wide football monitoring (after starting January 2023) because a 25-30 minute per-group session could not be sustained across 120+ athletes without large, uncontrolled gaps in the data, and staff turnover ended the coordination needed to run it at scale. The decision rule stated: keep a demanding test only where you have "smaller numbers, clear clinical purpose, dedicated oversight" (now used for targeted return-to-play groups paired with joint-specific isometric testing); drop it where you can't guarantee consistent, comparable collection. **Explicit** [2026-02-20-sportsmith-6otb-nordbord-er.md]

## Load monitoring and GPS

- The core GPS technology stack is Catapult (device/OpenField software) with Kitman Labs as the central athlete-management system for cross-technology storage, scaling, and visualization. **Explicit** [baylor-applied-performance-playbook.md p.7]
- Core GPS metrics tracked: maximum velocity, total distance, player load, field time, heart-rate bands, velocity/acceleration/deceleration effort bands, and Inertial Movement Analysis (IMA) for indoor sessions without GPS coverage. **Explicit** [baylor-applied-performance-playbook.md pp.11-13, Data Dictionary]
- A documented step-by-step SOP exists for the full GPS session lifecycle: charging, alarm-setting, activity creation, live period management, post-session sync, data cleaning (spike detection), tagging, and PDF export/reporting. **Explicit** [baylor-applied-performance-playbook.md pp.9-11]
- No sport-specific or position-specific GPS/load thresholds currently exist in the documented data dictionary — it is generic and vendor-organized, and building contextualized per-sport thresholds is named as unfinished, prioritized work. **Explicit (gap, stated as a gap)** [performance-playbook-development-plan.md 3.2.3, Phase 2]
- Heavy GPS/load-monitoring data was described as having bred a "culture of fear" in the field broadly (do less, to protect recovery), which the course material argues actually leaves athletes under-prepared for competition. **Inferred (transcript)** [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md pp.9-10]
- Daily subjective wellness surveys, rolled out across multiple sports via the AMS app starting summer 2022, were abandoned after roughly one academic year because athletes learned to game the consistent, low-effort answers the survey rewarded; the data looked clean but carried no real signal. Wellness surveys are still used, deliberately, in return-to-play scenarios and with athletes managing chronic health conditions, where subjective context still adds value objective data can't supply. **Explicit** [2026-02-19-sportsmith-6otb-er.md]

## Speed and sprint

- Linear sprint testing uses a 2-point start for team-sport athletes (for reliability), athlete self-selected start timing, at least two maximal-effort reps over 30-40 meters. **Inferred (author uncertain)** [ap-assessment-manual-draft-v12.md]
- Split times are interpreted by physical quality, not just total time: 0-10m is read as an acceleration score (reflecting maximal strength/power/slow stretch-shortening-cycle development), 30-40m as a speed score (reflecting sprint-specific/fast stretch-shortening-cycle development), and 40m peak velocity is derived from the 30-40m split divided by 10. **Inferred (author uncertain)** [ap-assessment-manual-draft-v12.md]
- SmartSpeed timing gates are the standard tool for sprint, agility, and reaction-time testing across the department. **Explicit** [baylor-applied-performance-playbook.md p.7]
- Speed is taught as the quality with the shortest ground-contact-time demand (~0.08s male, ~0.10s female at max velocity) and is described as harder to train and more genetically constrained than strength or power, but as the biggest separator between good and great athletes at the elite level. **Inferred (transcript)** [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md p.11]

## Strength and power

- The Vermeil hierarchy of athletic development (work capacity, then strength, then power, then speed, then sport skill, each level a required foundation for the next) is repeatedly described as the department's programming "compass." **Inferred (transcript + shared course material)** [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md pp.9-10; week1-lecture.md pp.23,26]
- Exercise selection is organized on a spectrum from general preparation through specific preparation, specific development, to competitive exercises (Bondarchuk), replacing the loose and often-misapplied idea of "sport specific." **Inferred (transcript + slides)** [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md pp.13-16; week1-lecture.md pp.32-36]
- The Principle of Dynamic Correspondence (Verkhoshansky) — matching amplitude/direction of movement, region of force production, dynamics of effort, rate/timing of force production, and regime of muscular work to the sport skill — governs whether an exercise actually transfers, rather than surface resemblance to the sport. **Inferred (transcript + slides)** [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md p.14; week1-lecture.md p.34]
- Training time is deliberately weighted toward high motor-unit-recruitment core/ballistic movements (squats, cleans, jumps, throws, sprints); single-joint assistance work is kept to a small share of total volume. **Inferred (transcript + slides)** [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md p.13; week1-lecture.md p.31]
- Off-season strength work prioritizes relative strength and technique first; already-strong athletes shift more time toward strength-speed/speed-strength work. In-season, the stated rule is "maintain intensity, reduce volume" rather than trying to build strength while managing a full competition schedule. **Inferred (transcript + shared slides)** [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md pp.20-21; hhpr-5355-class-8-2026.md p.21]
- Undulating/non-linear periodization (Parker/Miller/Panariello), waving volume week to week (e.g., medium-high, medium, high, low over four weeks) is stated as the personally preferred model over strict linear progression, specifically to avoid staleness while still building tolerance for higher loads. **Inferred (transcript, speaker unconfirmed but first-person: "For me personally, this is a more preferential model to use")** [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md pp.24-26]
- Prilepin's chart (total reps and reps-per-set by %1RM) is the default guide for daily rep/set assignment within a training block, used consistently across strength and power teaching. **Inferred/Explicit (shared team material)** [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md p.19; hhpr-5355-class-8-2026.md p.15]
- Strength's residual training effect is treated as roughly 30 days — a minimal retention dose is still trained within that window, even during lower-strength-emphasis blocks. **Inferred (transcript)** [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md pp.24-25]
- Tracy Fober's maxim ("use a barbell like a chisel, not a sledgehammer") is adopted as a guiding standard: prioritize movement quality and confidence-through-competence over chasing hypertrophy or 1RM numbers, especially early in a training cycle. **Inferred (transcript + slides)** [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md p.21; week1-lecture.md p.46]

## Conditioning and energy systems

- Conditioning is defined two ways: (1) how well an athlete can meet the energy requirements of their specific sport/activity, and (2) how well they sustain strength, speed, and power output across a full competition. **Explicit** [week3-lecture.md p.22]
- All three energy systems (alactic ~10-12s highest power, glycolytic/anaerobic-lactic ~60-90s medium capacity, aerobic hours-long lowest power/highest capacity) are always active simultaneously; programming targets the adaptation, not the drill label. **Explicit** [week3-lecture.md p.19; class-4-2026.md p.15]
- A sport cannot maximize all three energy systems at once — priority has to be picked based on what the competition actually demands (e.g., for football: prepared for fall camp first, then the first game). **Explicit** [class-4-2026.md p.31]
- For football specifically, the stated priority stack is: Goal 1, alactic and aerobic capacity/power for fall-camp demands; Goal 2, the same two systems for game demands; glycolytic conditioning is treated as a "worst case scenario" to be absorbed incidentally through camp and practice rather than programmed as a primary target. **Explicit** [class-4-2026.md pp.34, 39]
- Anaerobic (lactate) threshold heart-rate zones are assigned by athlete body size rather than a single number: roughly 165 bpm for big/tall athletes, 170 for medium, 175 for small, with Zone 1-4 bands set as percentages of that individual threshold. **Explicit** [class-4-2026.md pp.28-29]
- The traits easiest to build (aerobic) are lost fastest under detraining; the traits hardest to build (alactic/speed) are lost slowest — this ordering is used to sequence what needs the most frequent re-exposure. **Explicit** [class-4-2026.md p.47]
- Training intensities between roughly 75-90% of maximal effort are labeled (via Francis) "no man's land" — too fast to build alactic power cleanly, too slow to build real aerobic capacity — and are treated as a zone to generally avoid as a primary training target. **Explicit** [class-4-2026.md p.48]
- "Everything we do is some form of conditioning": lifting, agility work, and skill drills each load a specific energy system (power vs. capacity, alactic/glycolytic/aerobic) and should be classified and counted as conditioning, not treated as separate from it. **Explicit** [class-4-2026.md p.31, tables pp.35-42]
- For speed-power athletes, the aerobic system's main value is inter-effort recovery (regenerating ATP between bouts), not distance capacity — a better-developed aerobic system means more available energy on play 10, 11, or 12 of a drive, not longer continuous running ability. **Inferred (transcript)** [2026-02-05-hp5355-class3-transcript.md p.9]
- Aerobic-system training is kept below anaerobic threshold with roughly a 1:1 to 1:2 work:rest ratio and a general speed ceiling around 4.5 m/s, specifically to avoid spilling over into glycolytic-system stress. **Explicit** [week3-lecture.md p.51; 2026-02-05-hp5355-class3-transcript.md pp.11-12]
- Joel Jamieson's *Ultimate MMA Conditioning* is recommended as the single best practical resource for energy-system development, on the basis that its methods transfer cleanly to non-combat sports despite the MMA framing. **Explicit** [week3-lecture.md pp.49-50; 2026-02-05-hp5355-class3-transcript.md pp.11-12]

## Rehab and return to play

- Applied Performance's stated role in return-to-performance is to supply information that supports Athletics Performance and Athletic Medicine's decisions on training and RTP periodization — a support/advisory role, not a stated final-decision role. **Explicit** [baylor-applied-performance-playbook.md p.6]
- KPI-threshold identification for diagnostic screening and performance testing is named as a departmental operating principle, but no specific RTP criteria, battery, or phased-return protocol appears anywhere in this corpus. **Explicit (principle) / gap (no criteria stated)** [baylor-applied-performance-playbook.md p.6]
- Daily wellness-style subjective monitoring, dropped for general roster use, is explicitly kept in return-to-play scenarios specifically because subjective context still fills a gap objective testing can't. **Explicit** [2026-02-19-sportsmith-6otb-er.md]
- NordBord hamstring testing, dropped for roster-wide use, is explicitly retained for smaller, targeted return-to-play groups paired with joint-specific isometric testing (ForceFrame-style). **Explicit** [2026-02-20-sportsmith-6otb-nordbord-er.md]

## Injury risk and prevention

- Injury risk is reduced, not eliminated, through sound progression of load, training means, and density, in collaboration with Athletic Medicine — an explicit refusal to claim injury prevention as achievable. **Inferred (transcript + slides)** [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md p.4; week1-lecture.md p.9]
- Bodyweight-control competency is treated as a marker of injury risk in its own right: an athlete who can bench 400 lb but can't do 10 clean push-ups has "something out of whack" that needs fixing regardless of their strength numbers. **Inferred (transcript)** [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md p.5]
- Progressions in exercise selection, volume, and intensity are kept smooth and gradual by design; abrupt, large changes are treated as a direct injury-risk driver, not just a training-quality issue. **Inferred (transcript)** [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md p.27]
- No formal injury-risk model (ACWR, tissue-capacity tracking, a biomechanical screening protocol with defined red flags) is attributed to Eric anywhere in this corpus; the detailed treatment of ACWR and injury-risk modeling that does exist (Clemson curriculum) is reference material only, not confirmed Baylor practice.

## Recovery and sleep

- Recovery capacity must be "earned" by training hard enough first; recovery technology (Theragun, Normatec, cryo) is treated as useful but not a substitute for managing training stress and dose correctly. **Inferred (transcript)** [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md p.9]
- Recovery appears in the department's own subcategory list of Athletic Performance (alongside strength, conditioning, speed, agility, power, nutrition, applied performance, sports psychology, RTP), but no specific recovery protocol, sleep target, or monitoring tool is described anywhere in this corpus. **Explicit (named as a subcategory) / gap (no protocol stated)** [week1-lecture.md p.11]

## Nutrition and body composition

- Performance Nutrition is explicitly a separate Health & Wellness unit from Applied Performance, owning individual nutrition consultations, supplement education, body composition and bone density assessment, and the training table/fueling stations. **Explicit** [baylor-applied-performance-playbook.md p.5]
- Body composition testing is capped at no more than 4 times per year. **Inferred (author uncertain)** [ap-assessment-manual-draft-v12.md, "Anthropometrics"]
- Anthropometrics (height, weight, muscle-to-fat ratio) are described as strongly associated with success in most sports and worth tracking even though they are owned by Performance Nutrition rather than Applied Performance. **Inferred (author uncertain)** [ap-assessment-manual-draft-v12.md, "Anthropometrics"]
- No nutrition decision rules, body-composition targets, or thresholds are attributed to Eric anywhere in this corpus; this appears to be a deliberate department-design choice (Performance Nutrition owns it) rather than an oversight.

## Coaching, skill acquisition and constraints-led approach

- A trained "coach's eye," built through repetition and attention, is treated as at least as important as any technology for daily assessment: "you don't need a fancy test or anything to do that. But you do have to have a good coach's eye." **Inferred (transcript)** [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md p.8]
- New movement patterns are expected to take roughly 3-4 weeks of practice to become efficient and low-energy-cost, which informs how much proficiency to expect when a new exercise or drill is introduced. **Inferred (transcript)** [2026-02-05-hp5355-class3-transcript.md pp.6-7]
- Movement efficiency (skill) is treated as directly reducing energy cost — elite athletes (cited examples: LeBron James, Deion Sanders) are described as outlasting and outperforming peers partly through tactical efficiency that lowers the physical output required to achieve the same result. **Inferred (transcript)** [2026-02-05-hp5355-class3-transcript.md pp.8-9]
- Basic movement skills and basic lifts are taught before anything advanced, on the observed premise that many incoming athletes have high sport-specific skill from early specialization but poor general movement competency. **Inferred (transcript)** [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md pp.4-5]

## Periodization and programming

- Macro/meso/microcycle structure is used as the standard planning language, with an explicit reminder that a "training week" does not have to equal 7 calendar days — it can be extended to fit training themes around a real competition schedule. **Inferred (transcript + slides)** [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md pp.17-18; week1-lecture.md p.39]
- The preparatory period is sequenced hypertrophy/work-capacity, then basic strength, then strength-to-power, each with defined intensity/volume bands (e.g., hypertrophy phase 50-75% intensity, 3-6 sets of 5-20 reps; strength phase 80-90% intensity, moderate volume), before a transition into competition. **Inferred (transcript + slides)** [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md pp.19-21; week1-lecture.md pp.43-48]
- In-season programming deliberately waves volume and intensity even when the practice schedule looks repetitive week to week, specifically to avoid training monotony for the athlete. **Inferred (transcript)** [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md p.21]
- Training-compatibility is an explicit constraint on programming: incompatible qualities (e.g., maximal strength work and glycolytic-capacity work) are not both targeted at full intensity in the same block — "you can't ride two horses with one saddle." Compatible qualities are trained together; the rest are trained at a retention dose. **Inferred (transcript + slides)** [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md pp.28-29; week1-lecture.md p.70]
- An annual plan is treated as a flexible roadmap, not a fixed contract: lay out the competition calendar, key non-training dates (holidays, exam weeks), and phase-by-phase volume/intensity waves, then adapt the route as circumstances change while still reaching the same destination. **Inferred (transcript)** [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md pp.22-23; 2026-02-05-hp5355-class3-transcript.md pp.13-15]
- Training residual effects set the retraining cadence: aerobic capacity and max strength both need re-exposure roughly every 30 days; speed needs re-exposure roughly every 5 days or tissue readiness for max-velocity work degrades. **Inferred (transcript)** [01192022-hp5355-class1-transcript.md p.27]

## Tensions and contradictions

1. **The task's own premise doesn't fully hold.** Both class transcripts (Class 1 and Class 3) are diarized entirely under a single Fireflies speaker label, "Chris Ruf," for every line — including Class 3, which the Spring 2026 teaching schedule assigns solo to Rash (`spring-2026-teaching.md`: "Feb. 5 | Conditioning - Alactic-Aerobic | Rash"). Fireflies likely defaulted to the meeting organizer's name instead of doing real per-speaker diarization. This means roughly 30,000 words of the richest, most first-person material in this corpus cannot be confidently attributed to Rash specifically versus Ruf or Wenger. Every transcript-sourced bullet above is marked Inferred for this reason, even where the underlying statement is a confident, first-person claim.

2. **Department reporting model is not internally consistent.** The Playbook (p.6) frames Applied Performance as a peer discipline inside Health & Wellness that "enables" Athletics Performance, Athletic Medicine, Nutrition, and Mental Health to answer their own questions. But Week 1 course material lists "Applied Performance" as one of many training sub-categories nested under Athletic Performance itself (p.11), and the same transcript narration describes Rash's role as "helping program and plan the training" — a support-staff framing, not a peer-department framing. Which model actually governs day-to-day authority is not reconciled anywhere in these documents.

3. **Coach's-eye-first versus objective-first is argued both ways.** The course material repeatedly argues that "you don't need a fancy test... you have to have a good coach's eye" and criticizes the GPS/load-monitoring era for breeding a fear-driven under-training culture. Eric's own SportSmith writing moves the opposite direction on daily monitoring specifically: he replaced subjective wellness surveys with objective force-plate mRSI because subjective self-report couldn't be trusted. Both share a skepticism of trendy or convenient data, but they land on opposite defaults (trust the trained eye vs. require an objective number) for the daily-monitoring question specifically.

4. **The Assessment Manual (V12) reads as an older document than the current technology stack.** It builds its 1RM-estimation protocol around GymAware, and its CMJ description references a "contact mat" for jump height. The current Playbook technology stack and the Development Plan both center velocity-based training on Perch, not GymAware, and the current Force Plate Testing V3 protocol runs all CMJ variants exclusively on ForceDecks force plates, with no contact mat mentioned. This suggests the Assessment Manual predates the current VALD/Perch-centered stack and may be a legacy document rather than the currently operative protocol — worth confirming directly with Eric before treating any of its specifics as current practice.

5. **Testing cueing instructions conflict across two protocol documents for the same test.** The Assessment Manual's CMJ instructions say explicitly: "Do not coach the strategy of jumping." The current Force Plate Testing V3 protocol gives testers specific verbal cues to give athletes during CMJ, squat-jump, and hop-test administration ("keep chest up," "land softly," "land stiff-legged on only the toes"). These may be reconcilable (safety/technique cues vs. jump-strategy cues are different things), but the two documents read differently on how much a tester should say out loud.

6. **Two different anaerobic-threshold heart-rate methods appear back to back in the same slide deck without being reconciled.** One slide gives an age-based formula (max HR = 220-age for men, 226-age for women, threshold at ~85% of that), and the very next slides give a body-size-based table (big/medium/small athlete, ~165/170/175 bpm threshold) as the operational standard used elsewhere throughout the conditioning material. Which one is actually used day to day is not stated. [class-4-2026.md pp.27-29]

7. **The Development Plan's own critique cuts against the corpus it's part of.** It states the current Baylor Playbook "has no educational component whatsoever" and reads "like a vendor product reference manual," even though Eric personally co-teaches a full graduate-level course covering much of the same material the Playbook is missing. The department's teaching capacity clearly exists; it just hasn't been captured back into the Playbook or a formal intern curriculum yet.

8. **The Playbook's NordBord documentation has not caught up with practice.** The Playbook's Technology Overview and Workflows still document a full, unqualified, roster-style NordBord data-collection SOP. The SportSmith piece describes explicitly retiring roster-wide NordBord testing for football starting after January 2023, keeping it only for small, targeted return-to-play groups. The written SOP has not been updated to reflect that decision.

9. **A personally-stated periodization preference is stated inside an unreliable-speaker transcript.** "For me personally, this is a more preferential model to use" (about undulating over linear periodization) is a strong, direct, first-person preference claim — but it comes from the same unreliably-diarized Class 1 transcript described in Tension 1, so it cannot be confirmed as Eric's own stated preference without his direct confirmation.

10. **Older vs. current unit-goal templates show almost no adopted content.** The Geyer and Skodinski Unit Goal Programming Standards documents are staff-authored templates for individual sport programming philosophy; Geyer's is mostly filled in (periodization model, weekly split, KPI tests for softball), Skodinski's is nearly blank (just the section headers). Neither is attributed to Eric, and there's no equivalent document in this corpus showing Eric's own answers to the same template questions — a real gap if Flint wants Eric's own unit-goal-style programming philosophy in his own words.
