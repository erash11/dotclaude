## Lessons from the Supertraining Chapter 1 review (2026-09-26: 30 FIX, 1 NO-GO of 41)

- **Keep the source's internal contradictions.** When a book says X on one page and not-X on another, record both with their pages and set `contested`. Never pick one side silently. The NO-GO entry reversed the book's SJ-CMJ strength-deficit rule because the book states it both ways.
- **Name the right voice.** In co-authored books, write "the authors" unless a passage clearly belongs to one author. A passage that names Verkhoshansky in the third person is Siff writing.
- **Keep `proven` rare and scoped.** Scope it to the replicated core (for example, "Hill's force-velocity curve"), never the whole entry, and mark any disputed sentence inside it. Four of eight `proven` ratings were lowered.
- **No mechanism the source does not give.** About a dozen "Why it works" sections supplied physiology the book never states. Label it "Inference:" or leave it out.
- **Do not add "well established" or "modern research shows" without a citation.** Name the paper, or delete the phrase.

## Read this first: round 1 was wrong about Issurin 2016 (do not re-apply its correction)

Round 1 told the fixer to re-ground `contested` in the principle and training-stage entries on the vault brief for Issurin (2016). The brief says concentrated-unidirectional evidence was "insufficient" for combat, team-sport and swimming preparation [its p.6] and "restricted when peak performance requires many interacting abilities" [its p.11]. The fixer applied this exactly. **The correction was itself wrong.** The original paper shows:

- The "concentrated unidirectional (CU)" model is **Verkhoshansky's**, not Issurin's. Table 1 is "based on Verkhoshansky [9, 10]". The p.6 findings (basketball, handball and soccer fitness gains with no game data; swimmers 2 of 3) all come from Table 2, "Effects of concentrated unidirectional bloc periodization training programs".
- Issurin's own A-T-R model is the **multi-targeted BP** version. Section 3 starts at p.7, and p.11 names the canoe-kayak, track and swimming lineage. The review reports it is "supported by the findings of 28 studies" [p.11].
- The review does not soften the 2008 critique of the traditional model; it repeats it. CU "has no advantage over traditional planning, because it does not allow the multi-peak annual design" [p.6]. The conclusion also reads: "It would be a regrettable mistake to think that the traditional theory of training should stay the same" [p.11].

So the 2016 review cannot "dispute" the 2008 line "does not work well for high-performance athletes". Both entries now misstate a library source.

**There is a real Issurin-vs-Issurin contradiction to use instead.** It sits on the principle entry's own When NOT carve-out:

- 2008 (PDF 44 / p.33): the traditional approach "has visible benefits for the preparation of low- and mid-level athletes ... medium-level concentration still provides sufficient stimulation".
- 2016 [p.11]: "proper analysis gives no evidence that amateur athletes have any serious restrictions when implementing highly concentrated workloads structured in blocks of optimal duration". It also cites a recreational-trainee study where the block program was "considerably superior".

**Upstream note (the fixer cannot act on it; flag to Eric):** the vault brief `research-papers/issurin-2016-...md` never says that "concentrated-unidirectional" means Verkhoshansky's model, which is why this misreading happened. Its Key findings should be annotated: "CU = Verkhoshansky's model; the multi-targeted (A-T-R) model is the one the review favours."
