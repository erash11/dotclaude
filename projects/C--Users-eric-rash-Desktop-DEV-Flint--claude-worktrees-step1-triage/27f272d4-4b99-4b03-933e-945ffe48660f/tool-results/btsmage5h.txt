## Lessons from the Supertraining Chapter 1 review (2026-09-26: 30 FIX, 1 NO-GO of 41)

- **Keep the source's internal contradictions.** When a book says X on one page and not-X on another, record both with their pages and set `contested`. Never pick one side silently. The NO-GO entry reversed the book's SJ-CMJ strength-deficit rule because the book states it both ways.
- **Name the right voice.** In co-authored books, write "the authors" unless a passage clearly belongs to one author. A passage that names Verkhoshansky in the third person is Siff writing.
- **Keep `proven` rare and scoped.** Scope it to the replicated core (for example, "Hill's force-velocity curve"), never the whole entry, and mark any disputed sentence inside it. Four of eight `proven` ratings were lowered.
- **No mechanism the source does not give.** About a dozen "Why it works" sections supplied physiology the book never states. Label it "Inference:" or leave it out.
- **Do not add "well established" or "modern research shows" without a citation.** Name the paper, or delete the phrase.

## Missing (important ideas in the covered pages that no entry captures)

1. **Sympathetic vs parasympathetic overtraining, and the Omegawave readiness case** (U3 L1 p.11, 15-27). Overwork types: monotonous, sympathetic ("prolonged stress response") and parasympathetic ("advanced stage ... neuroendocrine system is compromised"). The p.17 table assigns the sympathetic form to "team sports, strength and power sports" (up resting HR and BP, up cortisol, down T/C ratio) and the parasympathetic form to endurance sports. p.18 gives a symptom table, p.11 the blunted double-exercise response, and p.19-26 a device-screenshot case that recovers with less training, better nutrition and time. Most football-relevant content in the course. Note the tension with U3 L2 p.12 ("T/C ratio ... not anymore" a gold standard) versus p.17 listing down T/C as a sympathetic sign. Confidence untested.
2. **Verkhoshansky (2012) activation reactions and the threshold of adaptability** (U1 L4 p.26-32). Training-load levels map to quiet activation, high activation, over-activation and stress. At over-activation, "the prevalence of anabolic process switches to catabolic", marking the threshold of adaptability and the critical level of homeostasis disturbance. Accumulated allostatic load past it produces allostatic overload. The homeostasis entry defers this to Supertraining, but no staged Supertraining entry contains "threshold of adaptability" or "over-activation" (grep).
3. **AIS (2013) body-mass by skinfold interpretation matrix** (U3 L6 p.10). Eight combinations of body-mass and skinfold change mapped to muscle-mass and body-fat interpretation (for example, mass up with skinfolds stable means muscle gain with no fat change). A directly usable tracking rule that sits beside the body-composition claim.
4. **Francis high/low intensity model** (U2 L6 p.19; U3 L5 p.23; U3 L8 p.5-6). High-intensity work separated by at least 48 h, a 35% high / 65% low mature volume ratio, sample three-high and two-high weekly schedules, and high-intensity distance requirements by sport and position (football back vs line). Evans uses it as the reference behind the WR weekly template.
5. **Specificity depends on mastery, and the performance chain** (U2 L4 p.19, p.27). "Principle of specificity will vary greatly according to one's training status ... and overall level of sport mastery"; low-mastery athletes transfer from nearly any modality. "The chain always breaks at its weakest link" across CNS, cardiac, energy, neuromuscular, ANS, hormonal and detoxification systems. Both qualify existing entries (weight-room transfer, Anokhin).
