## Lessons from the Supertraining Chapter 1 review (2026-09-26: 30 FIX, 1 NO-GO of 41)

- **Keep the source's internal contradictions.** When a book says X on one page and not-X on another, record both with their pages and set `contested`. Never pick one side silently. The NO-GO entry reversed the book's SJ-CMJ strength-deficit rule because the book states it both ways.
- **Name the right voice.** In co-authored books, write "the authors" unless a passage clearly belongs to one author. A passage that names Verkhoshansky in the third person is Siff writing.
- **Keep `proven` rare and scoped.** Scope it to the replicated core (for example, "Hill's force-velocity curve"), never the whole entry, and mark any disputed sentence inside it. Four of eight `proven` ratings were lowered.
- **No mechanism the source does not give.** About a dozen "Why it works" sections supplied physiology the book never states. Label it "Inference:" or leave it out.
- **Do not add "well established" or "modern research shows" without a citation.** Name the paper, or delete the phrase.

## Lessons from the 2026-09-27 review wave (Supertraining Ch2-3, Issurin Ch3, Bondarchuk, Evans, Old Bull)

- **OCR confidence scores miss the worst errors** (checked 2026-09-27). Zatsiorsky 2e: the OCR interleaves the two columns and the sidebars line by line, so its text is not readable prose; use it only to find content, and read and quote from the page image. *CFTS*: single-column prose that mostly reads in order, but upside-down lines come out as gibberish, some lines are dropped, and figures are scrambled, all at "0 low-confidence pages". ## Missing (important ideas in PDF 212-217 no entry captures)

1. **Small vs large loads change what else improves** (PDF 213 / p.202): "In those cases where training is conducted with small loads, there is a concurrent increase in endurance and speed of movement, along with the increase in strength, under loaded and unloaded conditions. If large loads are used in training, strength increases significantly, as does the speed of explosive movement. However, endurance under unloaded conditions begins to diminish and can even drop below initial levels." Candidate claim entry; relevant to in-season football strength work.
2. **Preserve rather than restore** (PDF 214 / p.203): "some of the training intensity should be directed at preserving the level of strength attained, which is preferable to restoring strength after it has diminished markedly." Belongs in the detraining entry (see its FIX) or as a short heuristic linked to Eric's 30-day re-exposure baseline.
3. **The weaknesses of current special strength training** (PDF 215 / p.204): beginners and highly qualified athletes use "essentially the same means", differing only in volume and intensity; this causes monotony and habituation, qualified athletes waste time on ineffective means, beginners use overload methods they are not ready for; athletes ignore "the qualitative specificity of the training effect"; and the opposite error, structural-similarity exercises "carried to absurd lengths", less effective than general conditioning. Conclusion: "any system of special training is deficient if it arbitrarily combines general developmental and special strength exercises." Candidate heuristic.
4. **Definition and evaluation of training effect** (PDF 215 / p.204): training effect is "the result of the influence of a means or complex of means on the body as revealed by the magnitude, quality and stability of the resulting adaptive changes"; it is evaluated by absolute and relative strength, muscular endurance, stability and temporal features, with stability judged by "the length of time for which it is maintained". Could be added to the taxonomy entry.
5. **Habituation vs overtraining as the two failure modes of a stimulus regime** (PDF 212 / p.201, opening of 4.1): "The same regime of stimulation can rapidly produce habituation and then fail to elicit any further adaptation, only maintaining the existing levels. Another regime can prove excessive and lead to overtraining... An optimum level of stimulation offered in a progressively increasing sequence of carefully chosen means is essential." The `_book.md` calls PDF 212 scene-setting with no discrete claim; this paragraph is a discrete claim. Check for overlap with Ch1.14 entries before adding.
