## Lessons from the Supertraining Chapter 1 review (2026-09-26: 30 FIX, 1 NO-GO of 41)

- **Keep the source's internal contradictions.** When a book says X on one page and not-X on another, record both with their pages and set `contested`. Never pick one side silently. The NO-GO entry reversed the book's SJ-CMJ strength-deficit rule because the book states it both ways.
- **Name the right voice.** In co-authored books, write "the authors" unless a passage clearly belongs to one author. A passage that names Verkhoshansky in the third person is Siff writing.
- **Keep `proven` rare and scoped.** Scope it to the replicated core (for example, "Hill's force-velocity curve"), never the whole entry, and mark any disputed sentence inside it. Four of eight `proven` ratings were lowered.
- **No mechanism the source does not give.** About a dozen "Why it works" sections supplied physiology the book never states. Label it "Inference:" or leave it out.
- **Do not add "well established" or "modern research shows" without a citation.** Name the paper, or delete the phrase.

## Lessons from the 2026-09-27 review wave (Supertraining Ch2-3, Issurin Ch3, Bondarchuk, Evans, Old Bull)

- **Invented Examples are the most common failure.** 34 of 36 Supertraining Ch2-3a Examples were unlabelled inventions. Use the source's own example where it gives one; otherwise start the Example with "Inference:".
- **"Verified against the page image" means you opened the image.** Four Issurin Ch3 entries claimed it for figures they had misread (Fig 3.7 inverted, a Table 3.7 row swapped). Sparse slides are not "bare diagrams" until you have looked.
- **Name the population and method the source gives.** Bondarchuk's data are 7,000+ native and foreign athletes, questionnaires, r >= 0.349, steroid-free; entries that said "Soviet athletes" were wrong everywhere.
- **Check what a cited paper actually says.** Issurin 2016's critique targets Verkhoshansky's concentrated-unidirectional model, not Issurin's A-T-R; Herzog & Leonard 2000 shows force enhancement, against the book's sentence. When the book misreports a study, record both.
- **No review history in entries.** "Prior version", "corrected from a NO-GO finding" and similar belong in `staging/fixes_*_applied.md`, never in the entry body.
- **Fix logs must match the files.** One fixer log claimed fixes that were not made. Fixers run their own grep checks and paste the output.
- **Section headers use the vault form** `**What it is** —` (with the em dash). Do not add suffixes such as ": mandatory". Em dashes are banned only in titles and body text. Never wrap a `[[wikilink]]` across lines.

## Evidence

- Every number, definition and quote carries a checkable locator: PDF page (and printed page if it differs), or file plus timestamp. Never write "confirmed in PDF" unless you opened that page.
- Never invent a number. Keep ambiguity visible: mark unclear audio or OCR as unclear, and do not harmonize two conflicting numbers from the source; record both with their locators.
- OCR sources (`staging/ocr/*.md`): pages listed as low_confidence in the header must be checked against the page image before any number from them is cited. Use the Read tool on the PDF page, or render it.
- **OCR confidence scores miss the worst errors** (checked 2026-09-27). Zatsiorsky 2e: the OCR interleaves the two columns and the sidebars line by line, so its text is not readable prose; use it only to find content, and read and quote from the page image. *CFTS*: single-column prose that mostly reads in order, but upside-down lines come out as gibberish, some lines are dropped, and figures are scrambled, all at "0 low-confidence pages". For any OCR source, quote only after checking the page image, and read every figure and table from the image.

Soviet-era or single-lab empirical findings and training prescriptions: `supported` at most. `untested` for single cases and anecdotes. `contested` where another library source or modern evidence disagrees (say which).

## Missing (important ideas in PDF 234-252 that no entry captures)

1. **Oscillatory isometrics (p.224).** Maximal voluntary tension in sinusoidally pulsed 5 Hz isometric jerks equals maximal sustained tension (Soechting & Roberts 1975). Basmajian (1978) reads this as showing the role of fibre recruitment and motor-unit synchronisation. This is the only evidence behind the "oscillatory isometrics" category in Fig 4.11. It could be one sentence in the framework entry (see that row).
2. **Static vs dynamic contraction differ in the nervous system, not the muscle (p.228).** "The difference between static and dynamic muscle contraction lies not in the muscle, but in the nervous system". Also on p.228: "Maintenance of a maximal isometric contraction ... depends ultimately on autonomic responses produced by muscle fatigue or protective reflexes". This is a conceptual claim with no entry.
3. **Research caveats for all regime comparisons (p.231).** Studies are "not very numerous", done "with athletes of low qualification", and biased toward "that method which is fashionable, such as eccentrics, 'cross training' or circuit training", so "anything definitive ... is premature". It is folded into the choice-of-regime FIX above but could stand as its own claim, because it qualifies every Ch4.2 comparative finding.
4. **Isokinetic quadriceps strength does not transfer comparably to squat strength, and the research gap (p.232).** "increase in isokinetic strength of the quadriceps does not result in a comparable increase in squatting strength". Few if any studies tested starting, acceleration, explosive or eccentric strength, strength deficit, or elite athletes. This is folded into the isokinetic-framework FIX. It is listed here in case the fixer prefers a standalone claim.
5. **Active visualisation with multi-muscle tensing (p.230).** Tensing several muscle groups while clearly visualising the sporting movement, "as opposed to the passive, relaxed visualisation recommended by many sports psychologists, can be useful adjunct to skills training, provided it does not alter the dynamic structure of normal competitive patterns." No entry has it. It could be one sentence in the loadless entry.
