# Passer-shooter pairs choose better shots the more catch-and-shoot attempts they share

**What it is** — Ben Alamar and Dean Oliver report that NBA passer-shooter pairs improve their shot selection as the pair accumulates catch-and-shoot attempts together. Taking a pair's value at 0% and following its first 200 catch-and-shoot attempts, the curve rises steeply: about 7% better shot selection in the first 50 attempts, then about another 4% up to 200 (o4rMbhsdk0I [09:43]). Players rarely get more than 200 such attempts in one season (o4rMbhsdk0I [09:43]). The pair does not reset to zero in the next season: the speaker says it starts "about five percent above where they started from", below the end of the prior season, and by the end of season two it is better than at the end of season one (o4rMbhsdk0I [10:14]). The units of the 7% and 4% are not defined in the captions (the measure is expected points per shot from their shot-quality model, so these are most likely relative changes in expected points; Inference).

**Why it works** — The speakers show three measurable drivers of the better shots: shorter two-point attempts, lower pressure on the passer, and more open shots, with about a 4% decline in pressure score over the first 100 shots (o4rMbhsdk0I [10:47], [11:18], [11:48]). They read this as the passer finding the partner sooner, before the defense reacts (o4rMbhsdk0I [11:48]). They do not test why the pair learns; the mechanism of learning itself is not given.

**When to apply** — As a prior for how fast a newly formed pairing should improve: steep early, then slower, with partial carry-over across seasons. Inference: for a new football pairing (transfer quarterback with new receivers), treat the first weeks of numbers as the steep part of a curve rather than as the pair's level. Eric's baseline: new movement patterns are expected to take roughly 3 to 4 weeks of practice to become efficient (course team position, speaker unconfirmed, Inferred). The talk agrees in direction but measures attempts, not weeks, so the time scales are not comparable.

**When NOT to apply** — The data are NBA catch-and-shoot attempts only: 1600 passer-shooter combinations with 10 to 50 attempts, averaging 1.12 points per attempt, 2023-24 season (o4rMbhsdk0I [07:38]). The speakers call the numbers rule of thumb (o4rMbhsdk0I [17:27]), say the results are statistically significant but noisy (o4rMbhsdk0I [21:03]), and say pair-level results are noisy and biased because one person typically did most of the passing to a shooter (o4rMbhsdk0I [21:33]). The effect is stronger for catch-and-shoot than for all shot types (o4rMbhsdk0I [24:07]). No age, rookie or role adjustments were made (o4rMbhsdk0I [35:23]). Football pass plays per quarterback-receiver pair are far fewer than repeated catch-and-shoot attempts (Inference). Do not use the curve to grade or rank individual pairs.

**Example** — The speakers' own example is the 2020-21 Phoenix Suns (see [[case-study--phoenix-suns-chris-paul-familiarity-ramp]]).
