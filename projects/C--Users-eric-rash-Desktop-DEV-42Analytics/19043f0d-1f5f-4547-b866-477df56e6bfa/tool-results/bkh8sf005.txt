## Eric's baseline
Eric's baseline: Applied Performance's stated role is to supply information that supports decisions made by Athletics Performance and Athletic Medicine, and to help the team answer and ask better questions rather than hand down answers. The panel extends this: coaches want analysts to arrive with context and a plan that can be communicated, and the panel's skepticism of "the result leads to the analysis" ([41:21]) matches Eric's baseline caution about judging a method by one outcome. Eric's baseline also says the annual plan is a flexible roadmap that gets adapted; the panel's scripted opening plays used to gather information ([21:15]) is a similar adapt-as-you-learn idea, but the corpus gives no matching football decision rule.
## Eric's baseline
Eric's baseline: Daily subjective wellness surveys were abandoned because athletes learned to give consistent answers that carried no signal, and objective measures (daily CMJ with mRSI) replaced them. The talk agrees on the weakness of self-report: the speaker says food diaries underreport and he tracks no intake. Eric's baseline also says data collection must justify its cost in time, compliance and staff attention, and that a valid test is worthless without consistent collection at scale (the NordBord decision). The talk leaves this open: the speaker describes morning fasted draws and a few days for results ([13:35]), which is a logistics cost he does not weigh against benefit. The Playbook also places body composition and nutrition with a separate unit, so this is a link to be routed through them, not a new Applied Performance data stream.
