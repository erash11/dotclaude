## Summary

The speaker, a former first analytics hire at the Pittsburgh Steelers who now works for Teamworks Intelligence, argues that college football analytics sits about a decade behind the NFL and that the gap is about to close. He says free agency, a salary cap and the transfer portal leave small scouting staffs with too many players to evaluate. He describes two tracking-data models his company built: change of direction (COD) and run-game credit attribution. Both are checked against film. He presents a per-game "digital scouting report" for every FBS player as a way to narrow large candidate pools. The claims come from the vendor selling the product.

## Summary

Ben Alamar and Dean Oliver study whether NBA teammates shoot better the more they play together. They build a shot-quality model from NBA tracking and pressure data, then regress shot quality and accuracy on counters of how many catch-and-shoot attempts a passer and shooter have had together. They report a steep early gain over the first 50 attempts, slower gain to about 200, partial carry-over into the next season, and roughly half the gain from better shot selection and half from accuracy. The speakers call the numbers rule of thumb and stress noise. Most of the talk is a long audience Q&A raising possible extensions.

## Key claims

- After acquiring Chris Paul, Phoenix started slowly, then finished the season strongly; points per attempt on shots off his passes started well below league average and improved. [02:00], [02:31]

## Summary

Ben Jenkins of Sumer Sports (also a PhD student at Florida Atlantic University) presents CLIP, a method that applies causal inference to offensive line pass protection using NFL tracking data. He argues that player credit is usually estimated as a residual from a prediction model, which relies on correlation. His alternative uses a doubly robust estimator with an outcome model and a propensity model to estimate, per lineman and per tenth of a second, the effect of allowing pressure on completion probability.
