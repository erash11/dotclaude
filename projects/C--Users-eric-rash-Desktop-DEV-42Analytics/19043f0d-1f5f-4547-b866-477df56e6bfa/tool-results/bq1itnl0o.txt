## Summary

A panel on how teams identify a franchise quarterback. Former NFL backup and quarterback coach Jordan Palmer says evaluation starts with confidence and maturity, then accuracy, which he argues can be trained and measured with vision testing, motion capture and an instrumented football. Kevin Mears, a football strategy fellow at Zealous Analytics, also puts accuracy first, says traditional stats hide too much, and names quarterback decision making as the open frontier. Both say fit with a team's system matters and that character, such as toughness, has no metric and was hard to read when evaluation moved to video calls. The panel is mostly experience and anecdote, with little data shown.

## Summary

A panel on how NFL teams blend tape, scouting and analytics in roster building. Rams COO Kevin Demoff, Texans GM Nick Caserio and ESPN analyst Brian Burke agree it is a hybrid model, not scouts against analysts. They say data is most useful for refining decisions retroactively, recalibrating scouts, and finding position-specific traits, and that the 40-yard dash matters little for some positions. Burke says combined metrics, such as weight with vertical jump, can show signal single metrics miss. Caserio expects growth in sports performance data used with players, such as force plates and strength asymmetry. Personality and character measurement is described as unsolved, and Burke urges caution with psychology research.
