## Summary
Benjamin Foster, a PhD student in environmental sciences and engineering, presents a method for valuing NBA draft picks that carry protections, conditions that let a trading team keep a pick if it falls in a protected range. He treats a protected pick like a financial option and prices it in basketball terms (five-year win shares), not money. The system simulates team standings, builds outcome distributions by pick position, runs a Monte Carlo simulation, and adjusts for market risk with the Wang transform. A worked example on one traded pick shows protections removing about half its value. The goal is to help teams choose protections.
## Key claims
- Draft picks are trade assets, and protections change the distribution of outcomes the receiving team gets [03:36].
- Millions of protection schemes are possible, so one base pick can have millions of trade values [04:07].
- The NBA trade market is illiquid, and protections can help teams assemble trades they otherwise could not [04:37], [05:07].
- Traded protections cluster at top five, top 10 and top 14, which the authors read as psychological anchors on round numbers [05:38], [06:08].
- Protected picks parallel financial options, where volatility of the underlying value matters [07:40].
- Risk preference differs by pick: the first pick is priced risk-seeking, and picks 25 and 30 risk-averse [14:48].
- In the worked example, protections removed about half the value of the base pick [16:20].
- The model's findings lined up with what the Suns general manager said at the time of the trade, per the speaker [16:51], [17:22].
- Applications named: reviewing past trades, private negotiation of protections, possibly spotting mispriced assets, and pricing other assets such as Bird rights [20:25], [20:56].
## Numbers and definitions
- Player value metric: win shares summed over a player's first five years [11:45].
- Team standings simulated 10,000 times for roughly 20 remaining games [09:40]; Monte Carlo simulation also run 10,000 times [11:14].
- Seven asset classes from K-means clustering of pick outcome distributions [14:18].
- One bar in the output: a 16.8 percent chance of a player producing between 17 and a half and 21 and a half win shares [11:45], [12:16].
- Priced values: 43.1 win shares per five years unprotected, 21.0 protected [15:49]. Implied annual 8.6 unprotected, compared to a named player's 2018 season [15:49].
- Protections subtracted about 51 percent of the base pick's value [16:20].
- A 78 percent chance the pick would be protected in the first year [16:51].
- Next-year outcome range for the fourth-worst team: first worst to 24th worst, median eight [17:22].
- Protected pick priced at 4.2 (annual win shares, as spoken), against 4.5 for the player received in the prior year [17:52]. Caption note: 4.2 matches 21.0 divided by five; the unit is not stated in the captions.
- Market price of risk (gamma) is the single Wang-transform assumption, read generally as the market's risk preference [13:47], [14:18].
## Methods and frameworks named
- Three principles: characterize volatility of the underlying asset, assess contractual conditions, account for market risk [08:40].
- Module one: Elo-based season simulation giving a probability distribution of finishing position [09:10].
- Module two: cumulative distributions of win shares by pick position from historical Basketball-Reference data, for first-round picks [10:42].
- Monte Carlo combining lottery probabilities and protection schemes [11:14].
- Wang transform, with market price of risk calibrated on pick-for-pick trades and interpolated where needed [13:17], [14:48].
## Limits and open questions
- Calibration of risk parameters rests on few pick-for-pick trades; the speaker says more work is needed [14:48].
- Simulation does not account for player movement, and the work was done on personal computers with limited time [19:23].
- The example comparison omits team-building considerations such as an expiring contract [18:22].
- Time discounting and option value of rolling a pick over are not explicitly modeled [22:26].
- Win shares was chosen for simplicity; other metrics could be swapped in [10:11].
- The explanation for round-number clustering is a hypothesis [06:08].
## Q&A
- Asked about discounting for player performance and the future option value of rolling over picks: the speaker said time considerations are hard to frame in a market sense and are not explicitly handled [22:26].
